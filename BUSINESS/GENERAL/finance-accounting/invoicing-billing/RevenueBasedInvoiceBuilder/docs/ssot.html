1. Purpose and governance

This document defines one tool and one set of invariants around invoices for a software product whose price is based on the customer's monthly ticket revenue - and it is the single source of truth for every decision we make about this tool, now and in the future. No decision is considered made until it is written here. Rules of use:

  • Every decision is logged here. Before or together with any change to this tool, the decision is added to section 11 with a date, and the affected section is updated in the same change.
  • The register is append-only. Decision rows are never deleted. A reversed decision is marked "Reversed (date)" with a reason - the original row stays for history.
  • This document wins ties. If this document or the code disagree, the discrepancy is resolved in this document first (with a dated decision), then applied to the code - never the other way around, and never silently left divergent.
  • Check before changing. Before changing any behavior covered here, read the relevant section and the register; after the change, update this document in the same change as the code.
  • Names must be self-explanatory. Every variable, function, and code snippet in any update uses understandable names. No cryptic abbreviations. Short names are allowed only for loop indexes and universally known abbreviations (id, url, api).
  • Acronyms are always expanded next to every use - in prose, tables, and headings. Literal identifiers stay as they are.

Core guarantee. The fee on every saved invoice is the waterfall sum computed from the pricing agreement at issue time, and a saved invoice NEVER changes when the agreement, settings or customer data change later - its fee, tax and total are frozen with a pricing snapshot.

5. Verified problem and root cause

5.1 Verified evidence

  • The pricing agreement (attached by the user on 2026-09-30) prices the software by the customer's monthly ticket revenue across seven brackets, with a waterfall rule: each rate applies only to the revenue inside its bracket.
  • The agreement's worked example computes CA $1,530 for CA $160,000 revenue. Its printed per-part numbers (CA $660 for the second bracket, CA $765 for the third) do not match its own rates (1.1% of $55,000 = $605; 0.8% of $85,000 = $680) - but the total CA $1,530 equals 300 + 550 + 680, which matches the rate-plus-cap interpretation.
  • The agreement's cumulative-max column also drifts from its own arithmetic: row 4 shows CA $3,885 while 300 + 550 + 1,000 + 1,800 = $3,650; rows 5 and 6 show $6,885 and $9,885 while the computed values are $6,650 and $9,650.
  • Before this tool, each monthly invoice had to be recalculated by hand, so the same agreement could be applied differently across months.

5.2 Current implementation (verified in code)

There was no implementation - invoices were produced outside the CMS. This document records the first automated implementation.

5.3 Structural flaws

  1. No single computational source. Every invoice depended on a human re-reading the table, so the agreement could be applied inconsistently.
  2. No freeze of issued amounts. Nothing prevented a later agreement change from silently rewriting what a customer already owed.
  3. Unclear cap semantics. The agreement lists both a rate and a per-bracket maximum per row without saying which wins; the worked example total only matches when the cap wins.

Root cause: the business rule lived in a document read by humans instead of in one deterministic calculation, and the agreement text itself contains internal arithmetic inconsistencies that must be settled once.

6. Requirements

  1. Compute the subscription fee from the monthly ticket revenue with a waterfall: each rate applies only to the revenue inside its bracket, and a per-bracket cap wins when the rate product exceeds it.
  2. Reproduce the agreement's worked example: CA $160,000 revenue yields CA $1,530 before tax.
  3. Charge the CA $300 minimum on the first $20,000 whenever revenue is above zero, with a per-invoice opt-out.
  4. Treat revenue above CA $3,000,000 as negotiated: the standard waterfall stops at $3,000,000 and the user enters the agreed fee for the remainder; saving without that fee is blocked.
  5. Freeze every saved invoice: fee, tax, total, customer details and a pricing snapshot are stored at save time and never recomputed from later changes.
  6. Number invoices automatically (prefix + year + running sequence) and support draft, sent, paid and void states.
  7. Export a printable invoice document and an email body from the frozen breakdown; allow PDF export and email through the CMS channels.
  8. Keep everything in the tool's own field value (no requestObjects), autosave after changes, and gate pricing editing and deletion to admins.
  9. Manage multiple customers (name, email, billing address) inside the tool.
  10. Provide one default waterfall used by all customers, with an optional per-customer waterfall that overrides it per bracket.
  11. Require a customer on every invoice and compute the breakdown from that customer's waterfall when they have one, otherwise from the default.
  12. Support custom line items per invoice (custom work, integrations, new one-time agreements) with a description and an amount.
  13. Support selectable support types with an optional monthly fee: a default in Settings and a per-invoice override; the support fee is a line on the invoice.
  14. Apply tax to the subtotal = subscription fee + support fee + custom lines, shown as separate lines.
  15. Produce a branded, professional printable invoice (header band, logo or monogram, grouped breakdown, totals block) and a matching email body.
  16. Allow uploading a company logo used in printed and emailed invoices.

8. Solution design

8.1 Core invariant

Invariant RBIB-FREEZE. The fee, tax and total of a saved invoice are written exactly once, at save time, from a calculateWaterfall call over the pricing brackets of that moment. No code path may ever recompute a saved invoice's amounts from current pricing - rendering uses only the stored breakdown and the stored pricingSnapshot.

8.2 The waterfall engine

// app/RevenueBasedInvoiceBuilder.js - calculateWaterfall, authoritative logic
var width = Math.min(rev, b.to) - b.from;          // revenue inside the bracket only
if (b.kind === 'minimum') {
  appliedFee = applyMinimum ? b.fee : 0;           // flat minimum, not proportional
} else {
  computedFee = round2(width * b.ratePct / 100);
  capped = b.cap > 0 && computedFee > b.cap;
  appliedFee = capped ? b.cap : computedFee;       // per-bracket cap wins
}
// negotiated bracket: rev > 3000000 flags needsNegotiation; the agreed
// fee is added on top of the standard total and required before saving.
  • Each bracket contributes its fee only for the revenue inside its range; widths never overlap.
  • All money values are rounded to cents (round2) at contribution level and again at the total.
  • The cumulative-max column is computed from the brackets (cumulativeMaxes), not typed by hand.

8.3 Caller contract table

CallerRequired change
liveRecalc (invoice form) Recompute from the SELECTED customer's brackets (bracketsForCustomer) while editing; never write to a saved invoice.
saveInvoiceFromForm Require a customer; store fee, tax, total, breakdown, customer snapshots and pricingSnapshot once; block save while the negotiated fee is missing.
renderSavedInvoiceBreakdown / print / email builders Render ONLY the stored breakdown and the stored customer snapshot; fall back to a fresh calculation only for legacy rows without a breakdown.
savePricing (admin) Update the DEFAULT brackets for future invoices only; never touch saved invoices or customer overrides.
saveCustomerModal Store the customer's own waterfall only when the default is unchecked; otherwise store null so the default always applies.

8.4 Customer waterfall resolution

// bracketsForCustomer: the exact resolution order for every invoice
var customer = findCustomerById(customerId);
if (customer && !customer.useDefaultPricing && customer.pricing && customer.pricing.brackets) {
  return customer.pricing.brackets;   // per-customer override wins
}
return DB.pricing.brackets;           // default waterfall otherwise
  • Unknown or deleted customer ids fall back to the default waterfall.
  • Deleting a customer never touches their invoices - the invoices keep the customer and pricing snapshots stored at save time.

8.5 Invoice totals with extras

// computeInvoiceTotals: the only way an invoice total is built
subtotal   = subscription fee + support fee + sum(custom line amounts)
taxAmount  = round2(subtotal * taxRate / 100)
total      = round2(subtotal + taxAmount)
  • The waterfall never changes: it only produces the subscription fee.
  • Support fee and custom lines are frozen on the invoice with their snapshots, like every other amount.

12. Maintenance rules

  1. This document is the single source of truth for every decision about this tool. Every decision is recorded in section 11 with a date, and the affected section is updated in the same change as the code. A decision that is not in the register did not happen.
  2. Rollback of a decision = mark it "Reversed (date)" with reason; never delete rows.

14. Open questions

Decision points we resolve together before or during implementation. Every answer becomes a dated row in section 11.

  1. Should the printed cumulative-max column (3,885 / 6,885 / 9,885) replace the computed values (3,650 / 6,650 / 9,650)? The agreement table disagrees with its own arithmetic; the tool currently follows the math that reproduces the worked example.
  2. Is the CA $300 minimum also charged on months with zero ticket revenue? Current behavior: no, zero revenue yields zero fee (D-RBIB-14).
  3. Should tax be applied by default? Current default is 0; Canadian installations will likely set 13% HST (or 5% GST) per instance.
  4. Do non-CAD customers share the CA-dollar bracket boundaries? Current behavior converts display only; bracket boundaries stay as configured (I-03).

17.1 Scenarios

  1. S-01 - Agreement worked example. Steps: click New Invoice, enter revenue 160000. PASS if: fee before tax shows CA $1,530.00 and the bracket rows show 300 / 550 (capped) / 680.

  6. S-06 - Numbering. Steps: save two new drafts. PASS if: numbers are prefix + billing year + consecutive sequence.
  7. S-07 - Print and email. Steps: open a sent invoice, click Print / PDF and Email. PASS if: the printable document opens in a new tab with the full breakdown, and the email flow reports the recipient from Settings.

R-01 Agreement table contains internal arithmetic inconsistencies

Applies to: the whole pricing engine (T-01)

What can go wrong: the printed cumulative maxima (3,885 / 6,885 / 9,885) disagree with the bracket arithmetic; quoting them to customers would contradict the computed invoices.

Likelihood / impact: certain to be noticed / medium (customer questions)

Mitigation: the tool always computes from the brackets; D-RBIB-17 documents the discrepancy and asks the user to confirm (section 14 Q1).

Last re-evaluation: 2026-09-30 - accepted pending user confirmation of the intended cumulative maxima.

R-02 Tax policy is per-installation and defaults to zero

Applies to: the whole tool

What can go wrong: an installation that forgets to set the tax rate invoices without GST/HST and under-charges tax.

Likelihood / impact: possible / medium (tax authority exposure)

Mitigation: defaultTaxRate parameter, per-invoice rate, and the tax line is always shown on the invoice.

Last re-evaluation: 2026-09-30 - open; confirm the customer's tax obligations at installation time.

R-03 Field value grows with every invoice

Applies to: persistence design (D-RBIB-13)

What can go wrong: many invoices (each with a breakdown array) approach the 1MB Firestore document budget.

Likelihood / impact: unlikely for years of monthly invoices / medium when hit

Mitigation: each invoice stores only its breakdown rows and snapshot (a few KB); revisit object-per-invoice storage (option D) if the record grows.

Last re-evaluation: 2026-09-30 - open, low.
