Restaurant And Menu Manager - Help

User help for Restaurant And Menu Manager. The single source of truth for how this tool works is its SSOT document (SSOT) - this file explains the same behavior for end users, in plain words, and adds where every field appears on the customer website. Written by hand on 2026-11-08 for this tool. The figures are drawn UI maps, not photographs; run npm run screenshots -- RestaurantAndMenuManager to capture real ones (the shot list lives in docs/screenshots/shots.json).

1. Overview

Restaurant And Menu Manager is the admin surface for one restaurant. You use it to describe the business, decide how it sells, build the menu, and create the offers the customer sees on the ordering website. Everything you enter here is stored as one document and read by the website food ordering widget, so the tool is the only place that has to be edited: nothing is typed twice.

It is written for the person who runs the restaurant, not for a developer. There is no publish button and no separate save dialog: the tool saves by itself a fraction of a second after you stop typing, and the website picks the change up on its next refresh. A field is either obvious (the restaurant name) or explained below with the sentence that matters: what it changes for the customer, and where the customer sees it.

2. Before you start

  • Where to find it. The tool runs inside the platform as an object-level editor for one restaurant. Open it from the restaurant record; the address bar and the nine tabs are the whole navigation.
  • Saving. There is no Save button. Changes are written about half a second after you stop typing. The small notice at the bottom confirms the save. If the notice says the save was blocked, a validation problem is listed in the same place: fix it and the save repeats by itself.
  • Read-only mode. When the platform opens the tool without write permission, every control is disabled, the Edit actions disappear and the summary cards stay visible. Nothing you see is lost; you simply cannot change it.
  • Try it safely. The bundled test harness (app/test-harness.html) has "Load Sample Data" and "Deal fixtures" buttons. They fill the tool with a demo restaurant, a demo menu and demo deals so every screen can be explored without touching a real record.
  • Read-only cards. On General and Tax & Pay the cards show a designed summary. Press Edit on a card to change it, Cancel to throw the change away, Done to keep it.

4. General - who the restaurant is

4.1 The restaurant profile (the hero card)

The top card is a hero, not a form. It shows the logo, the restaurant name, the brand line, the cuisine labels, the account status and three contact tiles that really dial, mail and open the website, plus a profile completeness bar. This is the block the customer recognises as the restaurant.

  • Restaurant name - the title on the website header and the name used in search results and receipts.
  • Brand name - the group or trading name, shown under the restaurant name and used when one owner runs several outlets.
  • Logo - upload or paste a link. It is the round avatar overlapping the hero cover, and the small mark next to the name on the website. A square image with a transparent background works best.
  • Cuisine labels - these are the filters and badges on the website and in the marketplace listing, not internal notes. Only the chosen ones are shown on the card; press "Select cuisine types" to open the picker, pick as many as apply, add a cuisine that is not in the list, and press Done. Cancelling the picker changes nothing.
  • Phone and additional phones - the first number is the tap-to-call number on the website and the number the kitchen is called on. Extra numbers are listed on the contact page.
  • Email - the address the order confirmations and replies go to, and the mail link on the website.
  • Website - the restaurant's own site, linked from the ordering page header.
  • Profile completeness - a hint for the owner, not shown to the customer. It counts the eight fields that make a profile usable: name, logo, at least one cuisine, phone, email, website, street address and legal entity.

4.2 Address and location

This card decides where the restaurant is, which fixes the delivery drawing area, the time zone used for opening hours, and the map point the customer taps for directions.

  • Street, city, ZIP, country, state - shown on the website contact block, in the order confirmation and on the printed ticket. The country also decides which state list is offered and which tax template can be applied later.
  • Find the address - type the restaurant name or the street and press Find. The search is limited to the country already selected, and to the state while "only this state" is on, so a name search cannot return a restaurant on another continent. Picking a result fills the street, city, state, ZIP, country and the map point in one action.
  • Time zone - the clock the whole tool uses. Opening hours, scheduled orders and special hours are read in this zone, so a wrong time zone shifts every time the customer sees.
  • Coordinates - the pin on the delivery map and the starting point for "directions". Pick on map, click the map or drag the pin, or paste a Google Maps link.

4.3 Legal information

Not shown to the customer, but printed on invoices and used by the platform for compliance. Legal entity name and VAT/tax number must match the tax registration; entity type and account status drive platform checks; the registered legal address is used when it differs from the restaurant address. Advanced fields add the company registration number, the data protection officer (worth filling for EU and UK restaurants, where the card warns you) and the legal address block.

5. Services - how the restaurant sells

5.1 Service toggles

These four switches decide which choices the customer is offered at the start of the order. Switching off delivery also hides the Delivery tab in this tool, because there is nothing to configure.

ToggleWhat the customer getsWhat also changes here
PickupA "pick up" option with a ready-in time instead of an addressScheduled pickup window
DeliveryAn address step, the delivery fee and the coverage checkDelivery tab, scheduled delivery window
On-premise diningDine-in ordering with a table numberOn-premise advance settings and payment channels
Table reservationA booking form with date, time and party sizeReservation card below
Anonymous orderingCheckout without creating an accountOnly available when on-premise or pickup is on

5.2 Opening hours

Seven day rows, each with one or more time ranges, so a lunch break is two ranges in the same day. Hours are read in the time zone set on the General tab. The tools on the card save typing: copy the first day to all days, set the same range for a group of days, and see today highlighted with a badge when a special hour or a temporary closure is active.

  • Temporary closure - one date plus a reason. The website shows the restaurant as closed for that day, which is what a holiday or a private event needs.
  • Special hours - a dated override of the weekday hours, for example a shorter Christmas Eve. It beats the weekday row for that date only.

5.3 Scheduled orders, reservations and on-premise

  • Scheduled orders - the minimum and maximum advance for pickup and delivery, and the slot interval. This is the list of times the customer can choose, so a 30 minute interval with a 30 minute minimum produces the next half hour onwards.
  • Reservations - party size range, how far ahead a booking can be made, how long a late party is held, whether a pre-order is allowed and whether a deposit is required plus its amount.
  • On-premise - the advance notice a dine-in order needs, in minutes, hours or days, and how many days ahead it can be placed.

6. Tax and Pay

6.1 Taxation

Three values decide the whole tax behaviour, and the summary card shows exactly them:

  • Tax name - the word printed on the receipt and shown in the totals row: VAT, Sales Tax, GST.
  • Tax mode - "added at checkout" means menu prices are net and the tax is added in the totals; "included in the price" means the menu price already contains it and the totals show the tax portion. Getting this wrong changes every price the customer sees, so it is worth checking once.
  • Delivery fee tax rate - the rate applied to the delivery fee itself, which can differ from the food rate.

Tax categories carry three rates each: pickup, delivery and in-restaurant. Use them when a category is taxed differently, for example alcohol. A category with all three rates at zero is shown as "0% everywhere". "Apply state or province template" fills a sensible set of categories for the selected state, and "Add category" adds an empty row. The category is then chosen per menu item on the Menu tab.

6.2 Payment methods

A matrix of four methods (cash, card, call for card, Interac e-transfer) against four channels (delivery, pickup, on-premise, reservation). A green combination is offered to the customer on that channel; a ghost one is not. The online gateway switch is separate: off means the customer pays on delivery or at pickup, on means card payments are processed at checkout.

6.3 Currency

The currency code used by every price in the tool, the menu, the deals and the website. It changes the symbol, not the numbers, so switch it before entering prices.

7. Delivery

7.1 Coverage zones

A zone is a named area drawn on the map, with its own minimum order and delivery fee. Draw a polygon, a rectangle or a circle, name it, set the fee and the minimum, and save. The map starts at the restaurant pin. The order is only accepted inside a zone, so the first thing to do on this tab is to draw at least one zone. A zone can be edited later without redrawing: press the shape, drag the points, save.

7.2 Address form fields

Each field of the delivery address (street, city, postal code, block, intercom, parking information, additional information) can be switched on and marked required. This is the form the customer fills, so only ask for what the driver really needs.

7.3 Custom fields

Extra questions with a label and a type, for example "Gate code" or "Leave at door". They appear at the end of the delivery form and on the printed ticket.

7.4 Searching the map

The toolbar searches addresses and business names and recentres the map. The pin button on a result writes the coordinates into the restaurant profile. The search is free and keyless (OpenStreetMap), limited to one request per second, and never used on the customer side.

8. Menu - what can be ordered

The Menu tab is three columns: categories and modifier groups on the left, the items of the selected category in the middle, and the item editor in the right drawer. Drag an item onto another category to move it, and drag a modifier group onto an item (or onto one of its sizes) to attach it.

8.1 Categories

Categories are the tabs or headings the customer scrolls through, in the order shown here. Each one has a name, an optional photo, a visibility mode (show, scheduled, hide) and a schedule when it is scheduled. A category with no items is not rendered, so it is safe to prepare one in advance.

8.2 Items

8.3 Modifier groups and options

A modifier group is a question the customer answers when ordering an item, and it can be attached to many items. Each group reads as one card in the panel:

  • Chips tell you at a glance how many options it has, whether the customer picks one or many, whether it is required, the minimum and maximum choices, whether duplicates are allowed, how many subgroups it holds and how many products use it.
  • Options carry the name, a price adjustment (plus or minus), a default mark, a hidden state and an out-of-stock state.
  • Subgroups are real groups nested one level deep, so "Choose a pizza" can contain "Extra toppings". They keep their own options and their own limits, and they are visibly indented under their parent with a sub badge.
  • Used in lists the products that offer the group. Expanding it is the fastest way to know whether a group is safe to edit or delete.

8.4 Importing and exporting

"Samples" imports a menu from a pasted JSON, a JSON file, an Excel sheet or a PDF menu. The importer reads the rows, shows a preview where each row is marked new, changed, unchanged or invalid, and only then writes. Append keeps the existing ids, replace overwrites the menu. Export writes the current menu to a spreadsheet so it can be edited outside the tool and imported back.

9. Deals - what the offer does to the price

A deal is the only thing in this tool that changes what the customer pays. It is a card on the website Deals page; opening it starts a guided flow where the customer picks the products the rule needs, and the discount is computed inside the cart. Deals are grouped by their Section, and the Active switch hides a deal from the customer without deleting it.

9.1 The eleven deal types, and when to use each

9.2 The four-step editor

  1. Deal type - pick one of the eleven cards. Each card names the type, describes it in one line and gives a concrete example, so the choice does not need memory.
  2. Offer setup - only the fields that type needs: the groups, the items or categories, the tiers or bands, the percentage. A match preview below shows how many menu items currently qualify, which is the fastest way to catch a filter that matches nothing.
  3. Details - title, description, section, price where the type uses one, image and the Active switch. The title is required before the flow lets you continue.
  4. Schedule - an optional date window, weekdays and time window. An empty schedule means always.

The footer drives the flow: steps one to three show "Next: <step>" and the last step shows Save Deal, so an offer cannot be saved before it has been configured. A failed save groups the problems by the step that owns each fix and takes you there.

9.3 Sections, scheduling and promo codes

  • Section - the heading a deal is listed under on the website, for example Family or Weekend. The field shows the chosen section and offers the ones already in use when you press Change section; a new name can be typed there. Leaving it empty puts the deal in the ungrouped area. Dragging a deal onto another section moves it; dragging inside a section reorders it.
  • Schedule - a deal with a weekday and time window only appears during that window, which is how a lunch offer stops at 16:00.
  • Promo codes - codes the customer types at checkout. Supported offers are percent off, a subtotal discount and free delivery, each with its own limit and validity.
  • Generate with AI - describes an offer in words and fills the editor. Each submitted request counts once against the monthly allowance shown in the toolbar; when the allowance is used up the actions disable until the first of the next month.

10. Deals and Promotions: which one?

Short answer. Use a Deal for anything that changes what the customer pays. Use a Promotion for a picture and a sentence under the hero, with no effect on the price. There is no third option: an older "campaign" record is simply a two-for-one deal, and the tool converts it automatically.

DealPromotion
Changes the priceYes, by ruleNo
Customer picks productsYes, inside the deal popupNo, it is an advertisement
How many can existNo practical limitAt most four are shown
Where it appearsDeals page, grouped by sectionUnder the hero image
Can be scheduledYes, dates, weekdays and timesNo
Works with a promo codeYesNo
Use it toSell more or reward a bigger basketAnnounce the deal you already made

Why only two? An earlier version of this product also had a "campaign" record type, but it could only express one offer: pick two products and pay for the cheaper one. That is exactly the "Two for one" deal, with fewer options, so the type was removed instead of being kept alongside deals. If a document still contains a campaign, opening it here turns it into a two-for-one deal with the same title, description, categories and size rule - nothing is lost and nothing has to be re-entered.

Rule of thumb in one line: if money changes hands differently, it is a Deal; if it only announces something, it is a Promotion.

11. Hero image and Promotions

  • Hero - the wide image behind and above the restaurant name. Upload a file or paste a link; a preset gallery is offered when the platform supplies one. One hero per restaurant, so it is the place for the signature dish or the room, not for a temporary offer.
  • Promotions - short advertising cards under the hero: a title, one line of text and an image. At most four are stored and shown, in the order listed, and the strip disappears when the list is empty. Drag to reorder.

13. Common questions

QuestionAnswer
Is there a publish button?No. The tool saves by itself about half a second after you stop typing, and the website reads the saved document. Watch the notice at the bottom for the save confirmation.
Why is a card showing a summary instead of fields?That is the read-only view. Press Edit on the card header (or the floating Edit on the profile hero) to change it, Done to save, Cancel to discard.
Why does the address search not find my restaurant?The search only looks inside the selected country and state. Check those two fields first, then search the restaurant name or the street address. Use "search the whole country" to widen it.
Why do my menu prices look wrong?Check the tax mode on Tax and Pay. "Included in the price" means the menu price already contains the tax, "added at checkout" means it is added in the totals.
Why is a deal not on the website?Three usual causes: the Active switch is off, the schedule window is not now, or the deal rule matches no item. The match preview on step 2 shows how many items qualify.
Can I reuse a modifier group on many items?Yes. Modifier groups are shared; attach the same group to as many items or sizes as you like, and the "used on N products" chip counts them.
How deep can modifier groups nest?One level. A subgroup cannot contain another subgroup, which keeps the customer flow predictable.
Why can I not add a fifth promotion?The website shows at most four, so the tool stops at four. Delete or replace one first.
What is the difference between a deal and a promotion?See section 10. A deal changes the price by rule, a promotion only advertises. An older campaign record is converted into a two-for-one deal automatically.
Why did my save get blocked?A validation rule failed, for example a deal pointing at a category that no longer exists. The message names the record and the field; fix it and the save repeats.
Does deleting an item break a deal?A deal that references a missing item is reported by the validator. Remove the reference on the deal before deleting the item, or keep the item hidden instead.
How many AI generations do I get?Thirty submitted requests per month, counted in the Deals toolbar and in both AI dialogs. The allowance resets on the first of the next month.

14. Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to do
The bottom notice says the save was blockedRead the message: it names the record and the field. Fix that field, then wait a second; the tool retries on its own.
Customers cannot order, they see "closed"Check today's opening range on Services, then check for a temporary closure for today and for a special hour that overrides it. Also confirm the time zone on General.
Delivery is not offeredCheck the Delivery toggle on Services, that at least one zone is drawn on the Delivery map, and that the customer address falls inside it.
The delivery fee or minimum looks wrongZones carry their own fee and minimum. The zone that matches the address wins, so check the zone that contains the pin.
An item is visible but cannot be orderedIts availability is "out of stock" or its visibility window has passed. Open the item and check availability and visibility mode.
Modifier choices do not appear on the websiteThe group must be attached to the item (or to each size). Open the item, find the modifier group checkboxes and tick it, or drag the group card onto the item.
A deal card shows but the discount does not applyThe rule matched nothing, the schedule window is closed, or the cart did not contain the products the rule needs. Check the match preview and the schedule.
Prices lost their decimals after importing a menuThe importer reads numbers as typed. Re-import with prices as plain numbers (12.99, not 12,99) or correct the affected items.
The address search returns nothing for a nameSome businesses are not in OpenStreetMap. Search the street address instead, then adjust the pin on the map.
Everything is greyed outThe tool was opened read-only. Nothing is lost; ask for write permission on the restaurant record.
Orders stop arriving at the kitchenOpen Devices and check the last check-in of the POS or kitchen display. A device that stopped reporting is usually a network or app problem on that device.
