What to hire one junior for, for 10 weeks
Document: JD-2026-06-DS | Prepared: 2026-10-28 | Version: 1.0

1. The question and the short answer

You have six candidate jobs for one person, one school-leaver, ten weeks, about 30 hours a week. This page compares them on the same five criteria, assesses the newest idea (testing UniconHub applications and every HTML tool), and ends with four packages you can pick from.

Short answer

Curriculum production is the best primary job. Testing is the best secondary duty. Curriculum work produces a finished asset that cannot break anything. Testing produces findings, not assets, but it is the one thing your own AI genuinely cannot do, because AI is never confused the way a real first-time user is.

Recommended package: B. Curriculum plus a full testing sweep, with tool building optional from week 8. It gives you two complete course packs, one approved outline, and one documented test pass over the whole library, at 3 to 4 hours of your time per week.

Do not hire her to do screenshots, demo data, catalog rows or document templates. Those are one command each. Attach them to the artifacts she produces.

The full management document (job card, 10-week plan, acceptance protocols, hiring test) is Job-Description-Junior-HTML-Tools-Assistant.html. This page is only the decision.

5. The testing idea, examined

You asked what I think about her testing the UniconHub applications and all the HTML tools. It is a strong idea with one condition, and it is not a full-time job.

5.1 What it is genuinely good at

  • Confusion testing. AI does not get lost, click the wrong thing, paste a paragraph into a number field or give up. A person does. This is unfakeable value.
  • Cross-tool consistency. Fifty tools built over months drift: different date formats, different empty states, different parameter behaviour. Only a person comparing them notices.
  • Permission and role reality. Testing as editor, viewer, manager and no-identity is a human judgement call at every step.
  • Evidence that survives you. A written defect register with reproductions is an asset the next developer can use.

5.2 What it is not

  • Not "click everything". Without a written test script per tool, the output is opinion, and you cannot accept or reject it.
  • Not a replacement for automated checks. Loading, console errors, syntax and screenshots are scripts and AI. She should never be paid to run them.
  • Not an asset. Testing produces a list. Its value depends entirely on whether you act on the list. If fixes wait three months, the money is spent and the value is not realised.
  • Not a good first month. A school-leaver in week 1 does not know what "correct" looks like. She will mark preferences as defects and miss real ones.

One condition before testing anything in the CMS. Testing applications means creating, editing and deleting real records. A junior must never do that in the live tenant. Either give her a separate test application, or keep her to the local harness and read-only views. No destructive action on real data, ever.

5.5 Verdict on testing

Good as a continuous duty. Weak as the whole job. Give her a 20 percent testing allocation from week 4, after she has produced something herself and understands what correct looks like. Then one full sweep lands around weeks 7 to 9, and every new tool gets a 45-minute regression pass from then on. Do not start the engagement with a full sweep unless you have known blocking bugs.

8. Why B, not C or D

QuestionAnswer
Why not testing first? Because week 1 she cannot tell a defect from a design choice. She will report 40 items, you will reject 25, and both of you lose confidence. After six weeks of producing and testing her own work, her findings are worth acting on.
Why keep curriculum as the majority? It is the only model that leaves a finished asset regardless of whether you act on findings. If the ten weeks end badly, you still have courses.
Why not tools? You can generate tools alone. What you cannot generate is independent acceptance. That is model 3, which is already inside B through testing.
What makes B fail? If you do not triage her findings weekly. A register nobody reads is the most expensive form of zero.
