Alternative titles: AI Content and Application Assistant, AI Content and Tool Builder

1. About the role

You work with AI as your main tool. Your job is to turn a request into a finished, checked artifact: a tested application, a small new application, or a complete piece of content.

The method is the same in all three cases: understand the request, research the source, write a short plan, produce with AI, test it like a real user, and report clearly what works and what does not.

You do not need to be a programmer or a teacher. You need to be careful, curious, and honest about what you do not know. We train you on the method, the templates and the checks.

2. The three work streams

Stream What you do What you deliver How it is checked
1. Testing applications and tools Run the written test list on an application or tool. Try empty forms, very long text, wrong numbers, wrong roles, phone width and reload. Compare similar tools against each other. A test report: steps, expected result, actual result, severity, screenshot. Your manager repeats a sample of your findings.
2. Building small applications Build a single-purpose tool with AI inside our template. No accounts, no external services, no stored personal data. The tool files, the test page, screenshots, and a five-line explanation of how it works. Automated checks, then your manager reviews every file before it is used.
3. Creating content Website content, education content or other content. Research the source, structure it, write every piece, and check that it is complete and correct against that source. A content pack: source list, structure, written pieces, a coverage list where the source has one, and review notes. Complete against the brief, sources cited, and an expert reviews the facts where accuracy matters.

3. How the three streams fit in one job

One job, three streams. The reason to keep them together is that they teach each other: testing makes you a better builder, building makes you a better tester, and content work forces the research discipline that both need.

Three rules that stop this becoming chaos:

1. One queue. Your manager assigns from one list, in priority order.
2. No more than two streams active in the same week.
3. One artifact at a time. Finish, test, hand over, then start the next.

Default priorityTesting commitments first, then the current content piece, then new applications.
Default splitAbout 40 percent content, 35 percent testing, 25 percent building. Your manager may change this monthly.
Why building comes lastIt needs the closest review and the strictest limits. It is not less important, it is less forgiving.

Mentioning all three is a good idea, as long as the priority order and the two-stream limit are written down. Without them, the role becomes three half-jobs and nothing finishes.

4. A usual week

  • Monday: a short plan for the week, agreed with your manager.
  • Tuesday to Thursday: production on the current artifact, one thing at a time.
  • Friday morning: testing and the written report.
  • Friday afternoon: handover, weekly summary, and the plan for next week.
  • Every day: three written lines at the end of your day: done, blocked, next.

7. Rules of the job

  1. Never touch git, a database, or publishing. Your manager handles those.
  2. Never edit existing tools or shared rules. Report instead.
  3. Never invent a fact, a rule, a date or a number. Use the source.
  4. Never test on live data. Use the test application or the local test page.
  5. If you are stuck for 30 minutes, ask.
  6. If you make a mistake, report it immediately. Early mistakes are cheap.
