1. How to read this

Fifty-seven curriculum ideas, each scored on six things that decide whether it can actually be built and sold: demand, regulatory ease, willingness to pay, source availability and licence, competition, and production effort.

Short answer

Build exam-prep and licence-knowledge products from official public handbooks. They are the only category where the PDF exists before you write it, the coverage matrix is objective, and the buyer already knows they need to pass something.

First three: US citizenship civics, CDL permit knowledge, and notary plus loan signing agent. Different buyers, all public sources, all outside the approval regimes that make real pre-licensing courses illegal to sell without a state contract.

Do not start with teaching, coaching or "how to become" courses. Start with "pass this test", because the pass mark is the product.

No statistics are invented here. The scores are my judgement from demand signals you can check yourself: job postings, exam registrations, licensing board pages, Reddit and Facebook study groups, and the number and quality of existing competitors. Validate any number before it goes into a sales page. The scores are a ranking tool, not market research.

1.1 The three filters every idea must pass

# Filter Why it decides everything
1Can it be sold without an approval?If the course grants a licence, a credential or required hours, it needs state or board approval. Exam prep does not.
2Does an official source exist?The source becomes the coverage matrix and the acceptance test. Without it, quality is opinion and the AI writes fiction.
3May you legally use that source?US federal works are public domain. Industry-body objectives are copyrighted but may be taught to. Reproducing them is another matter.

2. The one rule that protects the whole plan

Your pipeline is: subject listing, a PDF per subject, upload, then AI generation inside Curriculum Builder. The tool reads the PDFs attached to a lesson and generates study content, slides, flashcards and quiz from them. That means the PDF is the truth. If the PDF is AI-written from nothing, the course is AI-written from nothing, twice, with no check anywhere.

AI may not author the source PDF. AI may format, summarise, re-level and restructure an official source. Every fact must be traceable to an official page. If a fact has no source page, it does not enter the PDF, and therefore cannot enter the course.

Practical consequence: the curriculum producer's job is mostly building the source pack, not writing lessons. Get the source pack right and the AI generation becomes a formatting exercise with a checkable answer key.

5. What to build first, and why those three

Course 1: US citizenship

Why first: the source is federal, public domain, finite and stable. The whole curriculum fits in about 24 lessons. The pass mark is public. Buyers are motivated and easy to reach through community organisations.

Differentiator: a second language version, interview practice scripts, and a mock test that mirrors the real one.

Course 2: CDL permit

Why second: a public manual per state, a clear permit exam, and a B2B buyer who buys in seats. The state addendum model gives you 50 upsells from one base course.

Differentiator: a state-specific question bank generated from that state's manual, not a generic national one.

Course 3: Notary and signing agent

Why third: low competition, high ticket, small official source, and a natural upsell into a signing business kit.

Differentiator: a full mock appointment with documents, plus a pricing and marketing module.

Why not CompTIA first

It is bigger money but far more content, faster-changing objectives, and crowded competition. It is the right course 6 to 10, not course 1.

6. The production pipeline, step by step

This is the workflow you described, corrected in two places that matter: the PDF must be an official extract, and it must be attached per lesson, not per course.

1 Subject listing 1 to 2 days

One row per lesson with: code, title as an outcome, the source pages that support it, and one acceptance line. This table is the course skeleton and the coverage matrix at the same time. Nothing is uploaded before this exists, because this is what makes the output checkable.

2 Source pack, one PDF per lesson 3 to 5 days per course

Cut the official handbook into lesson-sized PDFs, typically 8 to 20 pages each, named 03-02-traffic-signals.pdf. Add a first page with: source document, version or date, page range, and the lesson objective. AI may format and re-level this, but every fact stays traceable to a page.

Why per lesson: the tool reads every PDF attached to a lesson and combines the text before generating. Attaching one 200-page manual to all lessons produces repetitive, unfocused content and wastes the context budget. A lesson-sized extract is the single biggest quality lever in the whole pipeline.

3 Upload and attach 1 day

Create the curriculum, sections and lessons in the tool, then attach the matching PDF to each lesson. Work in the local harness and hand the owner the pack; the owner imports to the live CMS. This keeps the no-database rule intact.

4 Generate 1 day for 20 to 40 lessons

Run Generate All per section. The tool produces study content, slides, flashcards and quiz per lesson, and skips fields that are already filled, so a re-run is safe. Generation is the cheapest step; the value is in steps 1, 2 and 5.

5 Quality pass 2 to 3 days per course

Four checks only: coverage against the matrix, every quiz answer against the source page, no duplicate or contradictory statements between lessons, and a full learner walk-through. A sample of five lessons is read line by line by a human.

6 Productise 1 to 2 days

Syllabus PDF, one free sample lesson, a ten-question sample test, a short outcome statement, a refund policy and an accessibility statement. Without these, a US buyer has no reason to trust a new seller.

Tooling caveat. The Curriculum Builder SSOT in the repository still carries template placeholder text, so it cannot guide anyone yet. Confirm the PDF attachment and generation flow in the harness in week 1 before planning 40 lessons around it.

7.1 What US buyers expect before paying

  • A clear outcome statement and a visible sample lesson.
  • A refund policy in plain language, and a working contact route.
  • Accessibility: readable contrast, keyboard use, captions or transcripts if there is audio.
  • No false claims. Never use official, approved, accredited or certified unless you hold it.
  • No trademarks of exam bodies in the product name or logo.
  • State sales tax handling for digital goods, which varies by state. Get advice before scaling.

8. What not to build

These look attractive and are the fastest way to a legal problem. All of them require an approval, an accreditation or a supervised component you do not have.

CategoryWhy to avoid it
State pre-licensing courses that earn credit hoursReal estate, insurance and mortgage pre-licensing need an approved provider in almost every state.
OSHA 10 or 30 cardsDelivery is restricted to OSHA-authorized trainers.
CPR, first aid and BLS certificationCertification requires an authorized instructor and in-person skills testing.
Food handler certificatesMost states accept only accredited programmes for the certificate itself.
CDL behind-the-wheel or ELDTEntry-level driver training must come from an FMCSA-registered provider.
Driver education for minorsState approval is required in most states.
CNA, EMT, phlebotomy, dental radiology certificationState-approved programmes and supervised clinical hours.
Any "become a certified X" promiseYou would be selling a credential you cannot issue.
Medical, legal or tax adviceRegulated professions; content becomes advice the moment it is specific.
Nutrition or therapy claimsHealth claim risk; needs a licensed reviewer and careful wording.

The safe formula: teach to a public exam or a public standard, sell the preparation, never the credential, and never imply affiliation.
