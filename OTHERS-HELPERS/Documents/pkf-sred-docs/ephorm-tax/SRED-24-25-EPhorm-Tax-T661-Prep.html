Line 242 - What scientific or technological uncertainty did you attempt to overcome?

counting...

At the start of the fiscal year, Ephorm Tax was an existing tax-preparation platform, and the remaining manual effort sat in coordinating clients, spouses, attachments, and prior-year records through email and static portals. Available tools could store documents, but none of them kept a multi-year tax workflow synchronized across linked records, messaging, and statuses in one place.

The main uncertainty was whether a multi-year workflow could stay consistent. Client records, spouse forms, accountant views, and previous-year data all feed the same filing, so we could not tell in advance whether updates would carry across without overwriting data, or whether partially completed forms and readonly states after submission could coexist with year-to-year copying.

Messaging raised its own questions. Accountants and clients exchange messages, files, and requests at the same time, and it was unclear whether unread counts, read states, and request statuses could stay accurate across sessions and devices without messages going missing.

Security was another open question. Tax data includes dates of birth, SINs, phones, and addresses, and it was unclear whether those fields could be encrypted in storage, decrypted correctly in emails and exports, and kept consistent across forms without blocking valid workflows.

Exports added a last uncertainty. Tax forms are dynamic, with conditional sections, uploaded files, and spouse data, and it was unclear whether PDF and ZIP generation would render consistently across browsers and keep working under concurrent export requests.

Line 244 - What work did you perform to overcome the uncertainty?

counting...

The multi-year work started from real failures. Copying information from the previous year brought data from the wrong year, the year selector stopped working, and clients were not told when previous-year information was missing. We reworked the year-copy logic so four steps copy per section, fixed the selector, and added an alert that lists missing previous-year information.

Linked records were the next problem. A spouse form could be completed without the spouse, copied addresses failed, spouse email fields conflicted with the client's, and spouse slips did not appear on the client side. Each link between client and spouse records was traced and corrected, including spouse date of birth and the rule for completing a filing without a spouse.

Messaging was rebuilt around read states. Messages did not appear on first send, unread badges could not be cleared or turned read, and the unread count had to come from the backend. We added read tracking, a badge that clears after reading, one-week filters with an unread option, and a central view so accountants do not have to open every client to see messages. Only the message owner can delete, and only accountants can send requests.

Security work covered encryption and invitations. Personal fields are encrypted across forms, and phone values are decrypted in emails. SIN and password validation were added, postal codes accept numbers only, and invitation emails gained expiry, auto-login links, resend checks, and a password restore path for expired links.

Exports were the last big area. Saving forms as Excel and PDF, zipping all attachments, and previewing files in the browser were built step by step, and production PDF failures were traced to the export path until it was moved to Puppeteer.

Form state was tightened: submitted forms become readonly for clients and fully open for accountants, save buttons were removed in favor of continuous saving with a prompt when leaving unsaved, and validation was written for conditional sections such as rental property, other income, and dependants. The production release went out in December 2024, followed by repeated testing of registration, emails, and frontend state.

Line 246 - What advancement did you achieve or attempt to achieve?

counting...

Ephorm Tax now keeps a multi-year workflow in one place. Information copies from the previous year per section, active and previous years carry separate statuses, and submitted forms turn readonly for the client while staying fully open for the accountant, with the year acting as the container for all state.

Messaging now runs on a read-tracking model: unread badges clear after reading, the unread count comes from the backend, a central view lists messages across all clients with one-week and unread filters, and clients are notified by email when the accountant completes their filing.

Sensitive fields are encrypted in storage and decrypted only where needed, such as in emails, and validation now covers SIN, postal code, and password rules without blocking valid entries.

Exports moved to Puppeteer with ZIP aggregation, so dynamic forms with conditional sections, uploads, and spouse data export as PDF and Excel with attachments in one download.

Open questions remain. Whether multi-year synchronization holds up under larger engagement volumes, whether read states stay accurate under concurrent messaging, and whether exports keep up as tax records grow are all still under investigation.
