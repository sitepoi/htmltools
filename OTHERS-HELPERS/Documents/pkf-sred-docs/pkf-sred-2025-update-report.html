SR&ED 2024-25 Update Report
What to update in each technical narrative before filing with the CRA
Date: 14 September 2026 | Fiscal year reviewed: 2024-25 | Version: 1.0

1. Purpose and Scope

Three technical narratives were reviewed:

  • SR&ED(24-25)EPhorm_Audit & JET_Version.docx - Ephorm Audit and Journal Entry Testing (JET)
  • SR&ED(24-25)EPhorm_Tax_Version.docx - Ephorm Tax
  • SR&ED(24-25)OpusChart_Version.docx - OpusChart

Each document follows the T661 Part 2 structure: line 242 (scientific or technological uncertainties), line 244 (work performed), and line 246 (advancements). This report lists, for each document, what must be updated so the narratives line up with the CRA's five SR&ED eligibility criteria: technological uncertainty, technological advancement, systematic investigation, technological content, and the exclusion rules for routine engineering and standard practice.

Priority legend: High must be done before filing, Medium meaningfully strengthens the claim, Low polish and consistency.

2. Updates Needed in All Three Documents

Priority Issue What to update
High Technological objectives are missing (T661 line 240) Each document starts at line 242. Add a line 240 section stating the specific technological objective of the project: what technical capability the work tried to create or improve, stated in technology terms, not business terms.
High No tax-year scope or dates Add the fiscal period the work was performed in, and state what existed at the start of the year (the base state). The word "continued" implies earlier years' work, which is not claimable. Scope every paragraph to work done inside the 2024-25 fiscal year.
High Base level of technology is not stated The sentence "we could not identify any available solution" is a market statement; the CRA requires a technological base level. For each uncertainty, add what a competent practitioner using standard practice could do, and why standard practice could not resolve the uncertainty.
High Routine-engineering language Words like "debugging", "query optimizations", "timeout thresholds", "retry logic", "frontend state management", and dashboard fixes read as routine engineering to a CRA reviewer. Reframe each as an experiment: state the hypothesis, the test, the observed result, and the conclusion drawn from it.
High Line 244 lacks a systematic investigation structure Rewrite line 244 so each uncertainty maps to a chain of hypothesis, experiment, observation, and conclusion. Add numbers: iteration counts, test dataset sizes, error or accuracy rates, concurrency volumes. Add approximate dates or month ranges inside the fiscal year.
High Advancements read as business outcomes Line 246 must describe a technological advancement: a new or improved technical capability, method, or algorithm. Replace phrases such as "reduced dependence on manual review" and "improved reliability" with a description of what the technology itself now does that it could not do before.
High Third-party integrations are over-emphasized Adaptation of existing technology (Stripe, DigitalOcean Spaces, Puppeteer, OpenAI APIs) is generally excluded. Keep integrations as context only, and move the focus to the internally developed logic: classification algorithms, synchronization engines, recalculation models, and permission resolution.
Med AI work needs careful framing Claim the engineering around the AI, not the AI itself: prompt validation frameworks, taxonomy design, output verification against user actions, and consistency testing. Exploratory AI work with no result yet must either be developed into a hypothesis-experiment-result story or removed from the claim year.
Med No supporting documentation Add a short paragraph in each document listing the contemporaneous records that support the claim: design notes, test logs, issue tracker entries, code repository, timesheets, and validation spreadsheets. CRA reviewers ask for these.
Med No personnel or skill statement Mention the technical roles that performed the work (developers, testers, subject experts) and the skills used, so the narratives tie to T661 Part 6 personnel details.
Low Closing "uncertainties remain" paragraphs Keep these. They support the argument that the systematic investigation is ongoing. Just make sure they describe technological challenges, not market or business ones.
Low Application names and project numbers Make the application names identical across the three documents and identical to the project titles on T661 Part 1. Add the project number once it is assigned.

3. Ephorm Audit & JET - Specific Updates

This document covers two different applications in one narrative, and the coverage is uneven: JET never appears in line 242, barely appears in line 244, but receives a full advancement paragraph in line 246. This mismatch must be fixed before filing.

Priority Issue What to update
High Two projects in one document Split into two project narratives, Ephorm Audit and JET, each with its own line 240, 242, 244, and 246. If they stay combined, JET must be given equal treatment in 242 and 244, not only in 246.
High JET narrative is almost empty Write the missing JET content: the uncertainty is normalizing inconsistent journal exports (QuickBooks and similar) so entries can be matched and validated without false positives; the work is the Python to Node.js migration with cross-environment output validation, fuzzy matching experiments, regex validation rules, and column mapping iterations; the advancement is the resulting data-normalization and validation method that tolerates structural variation between exports.
High AI validation lacks measurable results State the technical problem precisely: mapping unstructured uploaded documents to a taxonomy of audit request types using system-defined tags. Add the measured outcome: classification accuracy, false-positive rate before and after tag descriptions were introduced, number of prompt iterations, and comparison of AI classifications against actual auditor actions.
Med DigitalOcean Spaces section reads as ops tuning Reframe as the design of a secure document-exchange layer: encoding and decoding of sensitive documents, automated folder structures, and deletion lifecycle rules that cannot remove active evidence. Cut the timeout and retry wording, which suggests routine configuration work.
Med Base state is not described Add what the platform could do at the start of fiscal 2024-25 (for example, manual review with shared drives and spreadsheets) so the reader sees where the year's work started.
Med Audit data security If encryption of audit evidence is in scope, add it: how encrypted documents interact with AI processing, downloads, and lifecycle management. Do not claim new cryptographic algorithms; frame it as system design for secure workflows.
Low "Ask AI again" feature Keep it, but present it as an experiment method: repeated prompt execution used to measure output consistency across runs, which is a legitimate systematic investigation technique.

4. Ephorm Tax - Specific Updates

Priority Issue What to update
High Puppeteer migration is framed around a third-party library Switching to a renderer is adaptation of existing technology. Reframe the uncertainty around the internal problem: producing consistent PDF and ZIP output from dynamic, conditional, multi-year tax forms with linked spouse data and large attachment sets. Describe the rendering model that was designed and tested, and cut the browser-compatibility wording.
High Multi-year synchronization needs a technical definition Name the specific problem: bidirectional propagation of data between client records, spouse forms, accountant views, and prior-year data while preserving partially completed forms, read-only submitted states, and conditional sections without overwrites. This is the strongest part of the claim; make it precise.
High Encryption storyline Keep encryption of written tax data, but describe the system-design problem: how encrypted fields stay consistent across form updates, accountant notifications, exports, and email while remaining renderable where authorized. Avoid implying new cryptographic algorithms were developed.
Med SIN and password validation are routine These are standard validation rules. Remove them as claimed work, or keep them only as security constraints that complicated the real uncertainty (encrypted data flow and role-based access).
Med Messaging reads as feature work Focus on the consistency problem: unread counters, request states, and attachment visibility staying synchronized across concurrent sessions and devices, and the state-management and notification-trigger design that was tested.
Med No quantified evidence Add numbers: concurrent export stress-test volumes, session-synchronization test scenarios, number of iterations on the export pipeline, and attachment counts handled.
Low ZIP aggregation Mention in one sentence only. Bundling files into an archive is standard practice and adds risk if presented as an advancement.

5. OpusChart - Specific Updates

Priority Issue What to update
High AI recommendation section is too weak to claim The current text says work "began", was a "prototype", and it is "uncertain whether" the output is useful. That cannot meet the advancement test. Either develop it into a real investigation (hypotheses about input representations and prompt structures, an evaluation method for relevance and consistency, measured results, and a stated advancement), or remove it from the claim year and keep it as a future-project note.
High Core uncertainty is described at business level The strongest material is the recalculation engine: budgets, remaining balances, utilization, and profitability staying consistent as assignments, dates, vacations, company types, and billing rates change. Name it as an algorithmic problem: a recalculation model that must resolve overlapping assignments, date-based rate versions, and group-based rate structures without contradictory results.
High Stripe integration is excluded-work risk Connecting to Stripe is adaptation of existing technology. Reframe: Stripe was only a data source; the investigation was the internal billing state machine, license enforcement, refund-approval workflow, usage tracking, and automated invoice reconciliation that had to stay consistent with payment events.
Med Rate and billing structures need an algorithm description Describe date-based rate resolution and group-based hourly rates as a designed method: how a cost is resolved when a resource has multiple assignments and rates that change over time, and how the model was validated against real project scenarios.
Med Permission model reads as configuration testing Reframe the centralized permission controls as a role-resolution engine: a method for evaluating combined roles, readonly restrictions, activation states, and license-based limits with conflict detection, tested across the function areas.
Med Bug-flavoured phrasing Replace observations such as "duplicate project records" and "incorrect dashboard totals" with the underlying experimental findings: synchronization order and filtering strategies produced inconsistent totals, which the tested models then corrected.
Low Add scale numbers Give the reader concrete volumes: number of resources, projects, rate changes, and vacation entries used in validation, so the difficulty of keeping calculations consistent is visible.

6. Pre-Filing Checklist

  1. Confirm the fiscal year end and the 18-month T661 filing deadline for the 2024-25 year.
  2. Assign each project a T661 Part 1 number and title; use the same titles in every narrative.
  3. Complete line 240, 242, 244, and 246 for every project, including the split Ephorm Audit and JET.
  4. Restrict every sentence to work performed inside the fiscal year.
  5. Run a vocabulary pass: remove "debugging", "bug", "tweak", "tune", "optimization", "standard", and "routine" unless the sentence explicitly contrasts them with the claimed work.
  6. Add claimant name and business number consistently wherever the narrative is filed.
  7. Attach the supporting documentation list (design notes, test logs, timesheets, repository).
  8. Verify no claimed work falls under excluded activities: routine data collection, routine engineering, adaptation of existing technology, or social sciences.
