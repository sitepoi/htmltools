1How the Ranking Works

Every jurisdiction gets four numbers, then one priority score:

  • Opportunity (1-5): population served by the anchor corridors, intercity trip density, e-commerce and parcel demand, and the price gap between slow couriers and express - the wider the gap, the more the cost-sharing contribution is worth.
  • Regulation difficulty - Model A, carpool (1-5, higher = harder): from the regulation guide. Carpool cost-sharing is easy almost everywhere, so this is mostly 1-2.
  • Regulation difficulty - Model B, cargo (1-5, higher = harder): the real differentiator. Cargo has no cost-sharing exemption, so this column decides where the cargo lane can launch early and where it must wait.
  • Competition gap: whether an incumbent already owns intercity cost-sharing there (BlaBlaCar dominates Europe and India and Turkey; North America has no dominant player - Waze Carpool shut down in 2022, Canada has only the regional Poparide).

Priority score formula

Priority = (Opportunity x 0.50) + ((6 - carpool difficulty) x 0.25) + ((6 - cargo difficulty) x 0.25). Opportunity is weighted double because regulation can be engineered around, but an empty market cannot. Scores are planning judgments, not measurements.

3Launch Waves - the Sequence

Wave 1 - Months 0-12: prove both models where everything is easy LAUNCH FIRST

UK, Ontario, Texas, Germany, Florida. All have big corridors, easy cargo rules (or workable broker structure) and no cargo incumbent. Texas and Florida additionally have no dominant carpool player at all - the cleanest wedge on earth. Start one corridor in one country, run the full risk procedure from the operations manual, then replicate within the same country before moving on.

  • Goal: 1 live country with 2-3 corridors, then replicate the playbook.
  • Expected result: proof that seats + trunk sharing works with real retention before facing BlaBlaCar directly.

Wave 2 - Months 12-24: big markets with one hurdle EXPAND

India, Australia, California, Netherlands, France, Poland, Turkey. India and Turkey have enormous corridors but low ticket sizes and grey legal zones - enter with local structure ready. France and California are giant but cargo is hard - launch the carpool lane first, cargo only after permits. Australia's lanes are very long (880 km), which suits trunk sharing against expensive couriers.

Wave 3 - Months 24+: smaller or harder markets SCALE LATER

Belgium, Brazil, Mexico, Spain, Quebec, Nordics, New Zealand. Valuable but smaller or with a hard cargo wall (Spain's light-vehicle authorizations, Quebec's CTQ permits). Enter carpool-first or wait until the cargo structure is built.

Wave rule

Never open a new jurisdiction before the previous one has repeat senders above 30%, disputes under 3%, and the full evidence pipeline from the operations manual running. Corridors are won one by one - the scoreboard is the shopping list, not a simultaneous launch plan.

4Deep Dives - Top 8 Priority Jurisdictions

1United Kingdom

Opportunity: ~68 million people in a dense corridor network; one of Europe's highest e-commerce intensities; standard couriers are cheap but slow, express is expensive - a wide gap for the cost-sharing contribution. Regulation: carpool legal on cost-sharing; no goods licence under 3.5 t - the cleanest cargo jurisdiction in Europe. Competition: BlaBlaCar exists for seats, nobody leads seats + trunk.

  • Launch lane: London-Manchester and London-Birmingham corridors.
  • Watch: driver "worker" status law (UK Supreme Court) - design the driver relationship first.

2Ontario, Canada

Opportunity: ~16 million people, Toronto at the center of 200-500 km lanes full of students, commuters and small businesses. Regulation: carpool legal; intra-provincial freight deregulated - cargo needs safety registration and courier insurance only. Competition: only Poparide regionally, and no cargo player - an open field.

  • Launch lane: Toronto-Ottawa first, then Toronto-London and Toronto-Windsor.
  • Watch: stay intra-provincial at launch - cross-province parcels trigger the federal extra-provincial layer.

3Texas, USA

Opportunity: ~31 million people in the Dallas-Houston-Austin-San Antonio triangle (130-400 km legs); huge intercity car traffic and same-day business demand. Regulation: carpool legal with state preemption (HB 100); cargo needs the FMCSA broker structure plus TxDMV intrastate registration - the standard US setup, but enforcement is moderate. Competition: no national intercity carpool app exists in the US since Waze Carpool shut down (2022) - the largest open market in the world.

  • Launch lane: Dallas-Houston first.
  • Watch: hold FMCSA broker authority before the first cargo trip.

3Germany

Opportunity: ~84 million people; Europe's largest parcel market; 290-575 km lanes with heavy business travel. Regulation: carpool on strict cost-sharing; no freight licence under 3.5 t but the courier business must be registered (Gewerbe). Competition: BlaBlaCar is strong on seats - the wedge is the trunk, which BlaBlaCar does not monetize at scale.

  • Launch lane: Berlin-Hamburg and Frankfurt-Munich.
  • Watch: strict rules if profit creeps into the contribution - keep the published cost formula.

3India

Opportunity: 1.4 billion people; massive intercity flows and a deep courier-price gap; cost-sharing culture already established by carpool apps. Regulation: carpool accepted; light courier easy (GST + insurance + local permits) though aggregator licences vary by state. Competition: BlaBlaCar India and QuickRide on seats - cargo is open.

  • Launch lane: Bangalore-Hyderabad and Mumbai-Pune.
  • Watch: low ticket sizes mean volume is everything; state-level licensing differs - pick one state first.

6Florida, USA

Opportunity: ~23 million residents plus heavy tourism; Miami-Orlando-Tampa-Jacksonville lanes with strong same-day demand. Regulation: carpool legal, statewide preemption; general freight lightly regulated - one of the easiest US cargo states. Competition: none on intercity trunk sharing.

  • Launch lane: Miami-Orlando.
  • Watch: tourism demand is seasonal - build the resident business-user base for the off-season.

6Australia

Opportunity: ~27 million people but enormous 660-920 km lanes where air freight is expensive and road couriers are slow - long distance is exactly where trunk sharing shines. Regulation: carpool cost-sharing permitted everywhere; light couriers need an ABN, insurance and vehicle standards only. Competition: no dominant intercity carpool player.

  • Launch lane: Sydney-Melbourne.
  • Watch: drivers must register for GST regardless of income (ATO rule) - bake it into onboarding.

8California, USA

Opportunity: ~39 million people; LA-SF (610 km) is one of the busiest intercity lanes in the world; massive e-commerce and same-day expectations. Regulation: carpool fine on cost-sharing; cargo is the hardest US state - intrastate motor carrier permit (CA number) and the strictest enforcement culture. Competition: no incumbent on intercity trunk sharing.

  • Launch lane: LA-San Francisco after the Wave 1 states prove the model.
  • Watch: enter cargo here only after the broker + state permit stack is tested elsewhere.

6Final Verdict and Launch Order

The recommended launch order in one line

1. Ontario (Toronto-Ottawa) -> 2. UK (London-Manchester) -> 3. Texas (Dallas-Houston) -> 4. Germany (Berlin-Hamburg) -> 5. Florida (Miami-Orlando), then Wave 2 India, Australia, California, Netherlands, France, Poland, Turkey.

The logic: Ontario and the UK combine the easiest regulation with strong corridors and no cargo incumbent - the fastest path to a working, safe, repeatable model. Texas and Florida are the US wedge where no national carpool app exists at all. Germany tests the playbook against BlaBlaCar on its home continent with the trunk as the differentiator. Every later wave reuses the same corridor playbook from the operations manual.

All population and distance figures are approximate and illustrative; regulation scores are from the companion regulation guide, which must be re-verified with local counsel before each launch.
