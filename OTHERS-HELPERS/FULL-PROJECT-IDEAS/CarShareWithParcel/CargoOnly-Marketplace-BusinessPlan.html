BUSINESS PLAN - CARGO ONLY EDITION
Business Plan - Working Name: TripCargo

Intercity Cargo Sharing Marketplace
(Cargo Only - no passengers)

A marketplace that connects people who are already driving to another city with senders who need a parcel delivered on the same route, same day. The sender contributes to the driver's trip costs - the parcel shares the trip exactly like a carpool seat would - and the platform charges a small service fee. Strictly cost-sharing, no commercial freight, no passenger transport.

Prepared: November 2026 Asset-light marketplace Cost-sharing model only Illustrative figures

Concept

P2P crowdshipping: spare trunk space in intercity car trips is matched with parcels that need same-day, low-cost delivery.

Revenue model

15-20% platform service fee on each cost-sharing contribution + booking fee, insurance markup, seller subscriptions, enterprise API.

Verdict

Conditional GO: real pain point, but only as a corridor-first MVP with a strong trust and insurance layer.

1Executive Summary

Millions of cars travel between cities every day with empty trunks, while people and small businesses pay high express courier prices or wait days for a standard parcel. TripCargo monetizes that unused space: a traveler posts a trip, a sender posts a parcel, the platform matches them and guarantees payment, tracking and insurance.

  • Problem: same-day intercity delivery is expensive or unavailable; standard couriers take 1-3+ days; small businesses and individuals have no affordable door-to-door same-day option.
  • Solution: two-sided marketplace for intercity trunk sharing. No fleet, no depots, no drivers on payroll.
  • Monetization: commission (15-20%), booking fee, insurance add-on markup, seller subscription, enterprise API.
  • Differentiation: only mainstream player focused on same-day intercity small cargo via cars already on the road; passenger ridesharing apps do not carry parcels at scale.
  • Key risks: cold-start liquidity, parcel trust/safety, insurance and commercial-transport regulation, thin unit margins.
  • Verdict: Conditional GO - launch corridor by corridor with strict parcel rules and an insurance partner; national scale only after proof.

2Problem and Market Need

Pain on the sender side

  • E-commerce sellers shipping between cities need fast delivery but express couriers are 3-5x the standard price.
  • Urgent items: documents, contracts, spare parts, medicine from family, keys, event materials, samples.
  • Fragile or odd-size items that standard courier processes reject or overcharge for.
  • Second-hand sales between cities (online marketplace deals) have no cheap, fast way to hand over goods.
  • Students and young people moving small boxes between cities (dorm moves, personal items).

Pain on the driver side

  • Fuel is the single biggest cost of an intercity trip; a solo driver carries an empty trunk that could earn money.
  • Frequent travelers: university students going home, weekly commuters, sales representatives, people visiting family.
  • Today the only way to monetize a trip is taking passengers, which requires comfort and social interaction - cargo does not.

Market gap in one sentence

There is no mainstream, same-day, door-to-door intercity parcel service at a price between "cheap but slow standard courier" and "expensive express courier". Cars already on the road are the only supply source that can fill this gap at near-zero incremental cost.

3Solution and How It Works

  1. Sender posts a parcel: pickup city, drop-off city, size/weight band, desired delivery date, declared value.
  2. Driver posts a trip: route, date, departure window, available trunk space (bags only / small box / large box).
  3. Matching: the platform suggests drivers on the route; sender selects, or auto-match by corridor and deadline.
  4. Verification and payment: phone and ID verification, secure in-app chat, sender pays into escrow.
  5. Handover: pickup confirmation with photo, live GPS tracking shared with the sender during the trip.
  6. Delivery and payout: receiver confirms with a code or photo; driver is paid, both sides rate each other.
  • Trust layer: ID verification, reviews, escrow, parcel insurance add-on (optional, 2-4% of declared value), support-mediated disputes.
  • Parcel classes (hard cap 60 kg): document (under 1 kg), small (under 5 kg), medium (under 15 kg), large (under 30 kg), extra large (30-60 kg, carrier confirms lifting). Nothing over 60 kg, ever.
  • Pricing as cost-sharing: a contribution by distance band + size band, modeled as a seat-equivalent share of trip costs (fuel + tolls share), capped by a published formula so it never looks like a freight tariff. Sender sees a fixed contribution before booking, roughly 1/2 to 1/3 of express courier prices.

4Target Market

Supply - drivers

  • University students driving home weekly or on holidays.
  • Weekly commuters and sales staff between major city pairs.
  • People visiting family with mostly empty cars.
  • Small van owners doing regular intercity runs.

Demand - senders

  • Online marketplace sellers (second-hand, handmade, parts).
  • Small businesses and workshops sending parts/documents.
  • Students and young people moving small items.
  • Individuals sending urgent documents or gifts.

Corridor-first strategy

Launch on 2-3 dense city pairs (150-600 km apart) with high daily car volume - for example a capital-to-major-city corridor. Winning one corridor proves the model; corridors are then replicated city pair by city pair.

5Revenue Model

  • Platform service fee: 15-20% on each successful cost-sharing contribution (main revenue). The contribution itself is a capped share of trip costs, not a shipping price.
  • Booking fee: small fixed fee per order to cover payments and support.
  • Insurance markup: platform earns a margin on the optional parcel insurance add-on.
  • Power-sender subscription: monthly flat fee for frequent senders in exchange for a lower service fee and priority matching.
  • Enterprise API: integration for e-commerce sellers to auto-book parcel contributions from their order flow.

White space

No dominant player owns "same-day intercity small cargo by cars already traveling" outside the USA. The niche exists, but the failures above prove it must be won corridor by corridor with strict trust controls, not with a broad launch.

7Positive Opinions - Why It Fits the Market

For the idea

  • Real daily pain: senders consistently overpay for express or wait for standard; willingness to pay 2-3x standard courier price for same-day exists.
  • Near-zero marginal cost supply: drivers earn from a trip they would make anyway; no fleet, no fuel subsidy, no idle assets.
  • Asset-light scalability: platform costs are software only; new corridors cost almost nothing to switch on.
  • Recurring demand: marketplace sellers and small businesses ship repeatedly, which is a retention engine that passenger ridesharing lacks.
  • Environmental story: uses existing trips, cuts empty-vehicle kilometers; strong ESG angle for partnerships (universities, municipalities, corporate CSR).
  • Less crowded field: far fewer funded competitors than passenger ridesharing; a corridor winner can defend a region.
  • Natural upsell path: the same driver base and corridors can later add passenger seats (the Plan B model).

8Negative Opinions - Why It May Not Fit

Against the idea

  • Cold-start chicken-and-egg: drivers will not list trips without parcels, senders will not post without drivers; both sides must be seeded simultaneously per corridor.
  • Trust gap: theft, damage, fake pickups and delays are common in P2P delivery; dispute resolution and support costs can consume the thin 0.8-1 EUR margin.
  • Insurance and legality: personal car insurance typically excludes carrying goods for compensation. Unlike passenger carpooling, cargo has no cost-sharing exemption in most jurisdictions - any payment can make the trip for-hire, so the platform must hold the right structure per market (FMCSA broker authority in the USA, vehicle weight thresholds in the EU/UK, CTQ permits in Quebec). See the companion regulation guide.
  • Cheap substitutes: standard couriers and bus cargo are very cheap; only urgent, fragile or door-to-door niches truly justify the premium.
  • Weak driver retention: most drivers travel occasionally; supply collapses between holidays, and driver loyalty is hard to buy cheaply.
  • Demand-supply timing mismatch: a sender needs delivery today at a specific hour; a driver leaves when they leave - matching windows are narrow.
  • Failed precedents: Nimber (2020) and Deliv (2019) show pure crowdshipping struggles without a strong niche or a retail anchor client.
  • High acquisition cost: low order values (5-10 EUR) leave almost no budget for paid marketing; growth must come from partnerships and referrals.

9SWOT

Strengths

  • No assets, low fixed cost
  • Driver earnings from existing trips
  • Same-day speed no courier matches at the price
  • Simple, self-serve product

Weaknesses

  • No brand trust yet
  • Thin per-order margin
  • Supply spikes only around holidays
  • Support-heavy disputes

Opportunities

  • E-commerce returns between cities
  • University and corporate partnerships
  • Enterprise API for sellers
  • Later add passenger seats (Plan B)

Threats

  • Couriers drop prices on popular lanes
  • BlaBlaCar or a super-app copies the feature
  • Regulation of commercial carriage
  • Insurance partners refuse to cover

10MVP Roadmap

Phase 0 - Months 1-2: Corridor proof

GOAL: 50 verified drivers, 100 deliveries/month on 1 corridor
  • Pick 1-2 dense corridors; define 4 parcel bands and fixed price table.
  • Launch a web app (mobile-first) with manual "concierge" matching - no algorithms yet.
  • Recruit drivers at universities and corporate campuses with fuel-cost pitch.

Phase 1 - Months 3-6: Trust layer

GOAL: repeat senders above 30%, dispute rate under 3%
  • ID and phone verification, escrow payments, photo-confirmed handovers.
  • Insurance partnership for the optional parcel add-on.
  • Ratings and reviews both ways; automated matching for popular routes.

Phase 2 - Months 6-12: Monetization depth

GOAL: unit contribution above 0.8 EUR, 2-3 corridors live
  • Power-sender subscription and enterprise API pilots with 5-10 sellers.
  • Referral program for drivers; holiday campaign readiness.

Phase 3 - Year 2: Expansion and option value

GOAL: 8-10 corridors, 8,000+ deliveries/month
  • Replicate corridor playbook; add van owners for medium cargo.
  • Evaluate adding passenger seats - the Plan B bridge.

Funding reality check

The model needs roughly 6-12 months of runway to win the first corridor before contribution margin turns meaningful. Seed-level funding or founder self-funding is the realistic path; the asset-light structure keeps the burn low.

12Final Verdict

Conditional GO - with corridor discipline

Market fit exists for a narrow but real niche: same-day intercity small cargo, door-to-door, at half the express price. The idea dies only if it tries to be national from day one or ignores trust.

  • Do: one corridor, strict parcel classes, escrow, insurance add-on, university/corporate partnerships.
  • Do not: launch many cities at once, allow cash payouts before delivery, or undercut couriers so much that margins vanish.
  • Scorecard: market need 4/5, competition intensity 3/5, unit economics 3/5, regulatory risk medium, cold-start difficulty high.

Next step after proof: reuse the same driver base and corridors to add passenger seats on the same cost-sharing principle - see the companion document CarShareWithParcel-BusinessPlan.html (Ride + Cargo edition) and the Commercial-Carriage-Regulation-Guide-By-Country.html for where each model is legal.
