Why volunteer here

A real role, with real students, that looks good on paper because it is real

Ten roles, and every one of them changes what a family can do next - in a lesson, in an exam hall, in a scholarship seminar or behind the curriculum. One reliable volunteer changes what a whole group of students gets.

  • 🎓
    Real work, not busywork

    Whatever the role, the work you do is used by real students. No busywork, no watching from the back - and when a student gets unstuck, you are one of the people who did that.

  • 📈
    You are trained and reviewed

    Onboarding, session plans, feedback after your sessions and a proper review at the end of your term. You finish better at explaining things than you started.

  • 📋
    You leave with proof

    Documented hours, a reference that says what you actually taught and how you handled it, and a letter of support for university, scholarship or job applications on request.

  • 🤝
    You join a small, serious team

    We run this like a job: clear roles, real expectations, honest feedback. Volunteers hear about teaching roles when the centre opens, before anyone else.

Open positions

Every role we need, and the time each one really takes

We are actively recruiting for all ten positions, so nothing here is a waiting list. Apply for one position, or say you are flexible and we will suggest where you fit. You can apply from any city in Canada: every role below is work we genuinely need done, the time stated is what it really takes, and the roles that include an in-person day say so on the card.

Online maths teacher

1 hour a week - at least one lesson every week

Teach the weekly free online maths lesson: prepare the topic with our lesson plan, explain it a second way when a student is stuck, and keep the group practising on the work their class is doing right now.

Good fit if you: know maths from grade 5 to grade 12, can hold a fixed weekly slot, and stay patient with someone who is stuck.

Mock exam organiser

About 6 to 8 hours per exam, and never more than one exam every two months

Run a monthly exam with the exam team from start to finish: print the papers, manage the online registrations, help in the classroom on exam day, collect the answer papers, check them against the answer key, enter the results into the system, then email and WhatsApp the families.

Good fit if you: are organised, comfortable working in the registration system, and free on the exam day you take.

Scholarship seminar presenter (English)

Research at your own pace; presenting hours agreed

Find the scholarships that are really open, add every detail to our system, build the pages of our shared presentation, then present them in English. Take the research and data part, the presenting part, or both.

Good fit if you: read the official page rather than a summary, and you either enjoy turning that into clear slides - or you are happy presenting them to a large online audience.

Scholarship seminar presenter (other languages)

Research at your own pace; presenting hours agreed

Keep the scholarship records complete and translate the presentation pages, then present the seminars in your language for families who are more comfortable outside English. Take the research and data part, the presenting part, or both.

Good fit if you: speak fluent English and one other language, are careful with dates and numbers in both, and are comfortable presenting online.

Self-paced course assistant

Online in a WhatsApp group, on your own schedule

Guide a group of students through a self-paced course: answer questions in the group, supervise how each student is moving through the subjects, and accept them for the next subject once the current one is finished.

Good fit if you: reply quickly on WhatsApp, keep track of who has fallen behind, and like explaining the same idea in more than one way.

Curriculum builder

Completely up to you - from 1 hour a week, more if you want it

Work from our long task list, using the curriculum tools we train you on: pick one atomic task, finish it properly, then pick the next. Every task is small enough to complete in one sitting, so you always know what is done and what is left.

Good fit if you: are comfortable with computers and online tools, stay with a task until the result is exactly right, and can carry on without being chased. No subject expertise needed - the task comes with its content and its standard.

Online lessons helper

One or more lessons - you decide how many you manage

Do both halves of the hour: message students and parents so they know the lesson is happening and remember the time, then join it and answer them in the chat while it runs.

Good fit if you: write to students and parents with confidence, get the reminders out on time, and are helpful in a live chat.

Academic writing guide

Hours agreed with the programme lead

Set the subjects, give the writing tasks to the students, and follow how each one is doing through the platform so your feedback lands where it is needed.

Good fit if you: write well yourself and can coach structure and editing without rewriting the student's work.

Career coach

Hours agreed with the programme lead

Guide one high-school student at a time: set the goal together, open their file, then work the defined tasks with them until the file is complete and they are ready to apply to their target or take the next step.

Good fit if you: have walked a university, college or trades path yourself, and can keep a student honest about deadlines without nagging.

Full role definition

Career path presenter (professionals only)

Hours agreed with the programme lead

If you already work in a profession, present it to students and parents from every angle: what the job is really like day to day, what you actually do, how the pay and the career grow, which courses, licences or certifications are required and how long they take, what gets you hired, what to prepare at school now, and where the field is heading - the honest version, not the brochure.

Good fit if you: work in the profession today rather than having only studied it, can talk plainly about the entry requirements and the trade-offs, and are happy to take questions from the room.

What you leave with

Proof you can hand to an employer, a university or a scholarship board

Volunteering is only worth the time if it shows somewhere. Everything below is written down, checkable and yours whenever you ask for it.

  • 📋
    Documented hours

    A record of the hours you actually gave, in writing, whenever you need it for a form or an application.

  • ✉️
    A reference that says something specific

    What you taught, how many sessions you ran, how you handled a student who was losing confidence. Not a line about being nice.

  • 🎓
    A letter of support

    On request, for a university application, a scholarship or a job, written by the person who watched you teach.

  • 📈
    A review at the end of your term

    What you did well and what to work on next, so the next application you make is stronger than this one.

What we expect

The standard that makes a reference mean something

  • Prepare before every session
  • Arrive on time, or tell us early if you cannot
  • Keep student information private
  • Take feedback and act on it
  • Complete the training for your role
  • Finish the term you commit to
  • Tell us when something is not working

Every position states what it really takes. We would rather have one reliable hour a week than a promise of ten.

Training and commitment

Everyone gets the training their role needs

You are not dropped into a role and left to work it out. Training takes time though, and some roles carry a heavier training load than others, so those ask for a longer commitment. You are told which before you agree to anything.

  • Training before you start: onboarding, how we run lessons and exams, the systems we use and the standard we hold.
  • Support after it: session plans, feedback after your sessions and someone to ask when you are not sure.
  • A longer commitment where the training load is heavier, so the time spent training is worth it for you as well as for the students.
  • The exact commitment, written down, before you accept a role.

Applying from another city? Most of the work is online, so you can volunteer from anywhere in Canada. The roles that include an in-person day say so on their card above.

How we select volunteers

Five steps, the same ones every applicant goes through

We treat this like a real job, because our students only get one chance at a good teacher. Everyone who applies gets an answer, and everyone who goes through the process gets a reason.

01

Apply

Fill in the form below: the position you want, your subject strengths, when you are genuinely free, and two people we may contact.

02

Short interview

A conversation by video: your experience, why this role, and what you are comfortable doing in front of a group of students.

03

Teaching sample

You teach us something. Choose a topic you know well and explain it as if we were the students, and we ask the awkward questions a real student would ask.

04

References

We contact the people you named and ask them the things a reference should actually answer: reliability, how you work with people, how you take feedback.

05

Decision

Accepted, accepted with a training plan, or declined. You hear either way, and if it is a no you hear why and what would make the next application stronger.

What we are looking for

  • Clarity: can you explain a hard idea without showing off?
  • Patience: what do you do when a student still does not get it?
  • Reliability: will you be there next week without being chased?
  • Coachability: how do you take feedback that is not a compliment?
  • Judgement: do you treat a student's mistakes and their privacy properly?

We would rather run a small, strong team than a large, unreliable one. Applying is not a formality: the interview and the teaching sample decide the outcome. A no is a no for this round, not forever - if you work on what we tell you, you are welcome to apply again.

Good to know

Questions volunteers always ask

Do I need teaching experience?

No. Some positions need a subject you know well, others are organisation and guidance, and the career path talk is for people already working in their profession. You are trained, and you are never left alone with a session at the start. What matters is that you can explain something calmly to someone who is stuck, and that you turn up when you said you would.

What happens after I apply?

A short interview, a teaching sample where you explain a topic you choose, and reference checks. Then we tell you the decision and the next steps. You hear back either way.

How many hours is it?

It depends on the position, and we state the real number on each one above: an online maths teacher gives at least one 1-hour lesson a week, a mock exam organiser gives about 6 to 8 hours on an exam and never takes more than one exam every two months, and a curriculum builder gives whatever they can, from an hour a week.

How long do I need to commit for?

Long enough to be trained and to finish what you started. Some roles carry a heavier training load, so they ask for a longer commitment than others. We tell you what a role needs before you accept it, and we never ask you to guess.

Is it remote?

Most of it. You can volunteer from any city in Canada: lessons, self-paced course support, writing guidance and curriculum work are all online. Exam days, seminars and career talks happen in person at community venues, so those roles need you to be able to attend.

Is there any pay?

Volunteer roles are unpaid and we say so plainly. Teaching roles at the centre, when they open, are paid roles, and volunteers hear about them first.

What if I am not selected?

We tell you why, and what would make the next application stronger. A no here is a no for this round, not a door closed on you.
