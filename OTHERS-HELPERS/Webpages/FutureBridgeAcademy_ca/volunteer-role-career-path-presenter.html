Career path presenter

Present your own profession to students and parents from every angle: what the job is really like, how someone actually reaches it, and what it takes to stay in it.

What you do

  • Present your profession: what you actually do in a normal week, not the job advert.
  • Explain how you got there: the school subjects, the courses, the licences or certifications, and how long each stage really took.
  • Cover the reality: the pay and how it grows, the hours, the parts people do not enjoy.
  • Say what got you hired, and what you would do differently if you started today.
  • Tell students what to prepare at school now, and where the field is heading.
  • Take questions from the room, including the blunt ones.

What you need

  • You work in the profession today. Studying it is not enough for this role.
  • Able to talk about entry requirements and trade-offs plainly, without selling.
  • Comfortable presenting to students and parents, and taking questions.
  • Willing to use your own path as the example, mistakes included.

How hours work

Hours agreed with the programme lead

Preparation plus the talk itself. You are asked before you are put on a date.

What you get

  • Training for the role, before you start
  • Your hours in writing, whenever you need them
  • A reference that says what you presented
  • A letter of support on request
  • A review at the end of your term

How to apply

  1. Send the volunteer form, choose this role, and name your profession.
  2. Short interview, then a presenting sample: give us the outline of your talk, and answer two questions a student or parent would ask.
  3. We contact the two references you named, then tell you the decision either way.

Training is provided for this role before you start. Roles with a heavier training load ask for a longer commitment, and we tell you what that is before you agree to it.
