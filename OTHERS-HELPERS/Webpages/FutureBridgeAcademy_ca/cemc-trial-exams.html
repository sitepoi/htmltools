The programme behind the paper

Canada's largest mathematics and computing outreach programme

Run by the Centre for Education in Mathematics and Computing, inside the Faculty of Mathematics at the University of Waterloo.

The CEMC's story began in 1963 with the first mathematics contest for students in Ontario. Today it writes 13 mathematics and 3 computing contests for learners in Grades 5 to 12, and more than 300,000 students from over 85 countries register for them every year.

These are not school tests. Every paper starts near the curriculum and then climbs, question by question, into problems that need a real idea rather than a remembered method. That is what makes a contest result useful: it shows how a student thinks when the method is not given.

The official contest is ordered by a school. Students cannot register for it on their own, and the paper is written under a supervisor. That single rule is the reason this page exists.

What students gain

  • A genuine interest in mathematics and computing, built on problems worth solving
  • Creative problem-solving, critical thinking and analytical habits
  • New concepts, and the connections between topics that school rarely has time for
  • Confidence from tackling hard problems - the CEMC's own stated outcome

The papers

Which paper your child sits

Our trial papers run from Grade 5 to Grade 11. Every sitting is one hour, and the paper matches the grade your child is in now.

Grade Trial paper What it practises Length
5-10 Beaver Computing Challenge Logic, patterns and computational thinking 1 hour
7-8 Gauss Number sense and multi-step problem solving 1 hour
9 Pascal or Fryer Speed and accuracy, or written reasoning 1 hour
10 Cayley or Galois Algebra and geometry under time 1 hour
11 Fermat or Hypatia Advanced problem solving and proof 1 hour

Every trial paper runs one hour. The paper is chosen for the grade on the form, and no preparation is needed to sit it. The sitting is practice, not the official contest.

Why we run a trial exam

Because the first attempt should not be the one that counts

Four reasons, all of them practical.

01

A contest is a different skill

A school mark measures a year of work. A contest measures one hour of thinking on problems nobody has seen before. That skill is trainable - but only if it is tested.

02

The paper sits behind a school door

CEMC papers are ordered by educators, not by families. If your school does not enter this year, the trial sitting is how your child still writes a full paper under real conditions.

03

The clock decides marks

Sixty minutes, one paper, no second pass. A student who has never sat a timed paper loses marks they already know how to earn - and never finds out which ones.

04

A score alone teaches nothing

The useful part is the marked paper: which questions cost marks, and which topics sit behind them. That is what turns a result into a plan.

Every sitting is recorded. Each family receives a dated attendance certificate naming the paper and the date - useful evidence of academic commitment for school files, IB portfolios and university applications.

On the day

Four steps, no surprises

  1. 1

    Register

    One form per student: parent name, student name, grade, email and a phone number for the day. It takes under a minute.

  2. 2

    Sit the paper

    In person, at the venue for your city, under contest timing. One hour, phones away, nothing else on the desk.

  3. 3

    Marking

    The paper is marked after the sitting. Multiple-choice papers are scored by question; written papers are marked for method as well as answer.

  4. 4

    The picture

    You get the score and the questions it came from - the part that tells you what to practise next.

Questions parents ask

The honest answers

Is the trial exam the official CEMC contest?

No. It is a practice sitting that we run, in the same style and under the same kind of timing. The official contest is ordered and supervised by a school, and CEMC sets its dates. The trial exists so the real paper is not your child's first one.

Which paper will my child write?

The paper that matches their grade: the Beaver Computing Challenge for Grades 5 to 10, Gauss for Grades 7 and 8, then Pascal or Fryer in Grade 9, Cayley or Galois in Grade 10, and Fermat or Hypatia in Grade 11. Tell us the grade on the form and we place the student on the right paper.

Does my child need to prepare?

No. Come as they are. The sitting is designed to show what the paper reveals, and a first attempt is data, not a verdict.

What if the paper feels too hard?

It will. CEMC builds every paper so the questions climb, and almost nobody finishes. A student who solves the first ten and attempts two more has done well.

How long will we be there?

About an hour. Every trial paper runs 60 minutes, whichever grade your child is in. Add a few minutes either side for arrival and collection.

Do we get a record of attendance?

Yes. Every sitting comes with a dated attendance certificate naming the paper and the date. Families use it for school files, IB portfolios and university applications as evidence of academic commitment.

Is this the same as your free monthly mock exam?

No, and we keep them separate. The monthly mock exam follows the topic your child is studying at school and is free. The CEMC trial exam is contest mathematics - different paper, different purpose, different sign-up.

Can my child sit the official contest through you?

The official paper has to be ordered by a school and written under a supervisor. If your school enters, your child sits it at school. If it does not, the trial exam is still open to you - and it is the closest preparation we can give before the next contest date.
