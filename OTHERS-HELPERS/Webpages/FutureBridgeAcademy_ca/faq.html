Money and what is free

The part most websites are vague about

Our rule is simple: nothing on this site has a price on it, and nothing is presented as free when it is not.

Are the online lessons really free?

Yes. The weekly online lessons are free for every family in the community, and the monthly mock exam, the scholarship seminars and the parent seminars are free too. That is the whole point of the programme.

What exactly is free today?

Weekly online mathematics lessons, a monthly mock exam held in person, scholarship seminars, parent seminars, the community channel and the volunteer mentor network.

Do you publish prices anywhere?

No, never on this website. Some optional programmes do carry a fee, and when that is the case we explain the cost clearly before you agree to anything - never after.

What if something is not free and we are unsure?

Ask us before deciding. We would rather spend five minutes explaining than have a family guess, and we will tell you plainly when a programme is not for you.

Lessons and learning

How the teaching actually works

Mathematics runs now. English and writing uses the same model and opens with the centre.

What happens in a lesson?

A warm-up on last week's work, the main part on the topic the class is on at school right now, guided practice together, then independent practice while mentors watch. Every lesson ends with what to practise before the next one.

What does my child need to join?

A phone, tablet or computer with internet, a quiet corner, and paper and a pencil. Nothing to install, nothing to buy. A camera and microphone help but are never required.

Do we have to come every week?

No. Come when you can. Every lesson works on the topic the class is on, so a missed week is a gap rather than a disaster.

Online, in centre, or both?

Online today: the weekly lessons and the seminars. In person today: the monthly mock exams at community venues. Small-group lessons and one-to-one mentoring open as the centre does.

How large are the groups?

Small, deliberately. The free online lessons are open to everyone in our community, and when the small-group lessons open we keep those groups small enough that every learner gets attention - that is the point of having a room.

What if my child is ahead of the class, or behind?

The plan starts where your child actually is. Being ahead means more depth rather than repetition; being behind means smaller steps, not less support.

Is it only mathematics?

Mathematics runs now, and it is our first subject. English and writing follows the same model and opens with the centre. Science is not offered at the moment.

The monthly mock exam

In person, on the school topic, free

Topic-based testing means the exam measures what was just taught, not what was memorised in a panic the night before.

Can my child sit the mock exam online?

No. Every monthly mock exam is held in person at a community venue. There is no online version, because the value is in the room, the clock and the paper.

Which topics are tested?

The section your child is studying at school when the exam runs. You tell us the topic when you register and the paper is built around it.

Does my child need to prepare?

No cramming and no revision book. The exam is designed to show what to work on next, so an imperfect result is useful information rather than a failure.

Do you do contests and competitions?

Contest mathematics (CEMC: Gauss, Pascal, Cayley, Fermat, Euclid) runs as its own track for students who want it, and it never replaces the core programme. The monthly mock exam is a separate thing entirely, and it is free for everyone.

Joining and progress

From the first message onwards

Starting is meant to be easy and leaving is meant to be easy. In between, you should always be able to see what is happening.

How do we start?

Send the short join form with your child's grade and the topic their class is on. We reply with the joining details for the next free lesson, and you decide afterwards whether to come back.

How will I know it is working?

Every month: a mock exam on the topic your child is studying, plus a growth report covering topics, work habits and the next goal. If something is not working, you see it as early as we do.

Can we pause, or change subject?

Yes. Mathematics comes first and English follows the same model, so changing subject is a plan update rather than a fresh start, and a pause keeps everything on file for when you come back.

How much practice is expected at home?

We do not publish a homework quota. Practice follows the lesson, it is always on the topic being taught, and you can see what was set. If a week is impossible, nothing collapses.

How do we leave the list?

One message. We take you off the list and delete your details, with no follow-up sequence and no exit interview.

Who runs the programme

An honest picture of the organisation

Future Bridge Academy is the programme's trading name. The charity and the partner organisations behind it are named openly.

Who runs Future Bridge Academy?

CentreCanada Newcomer Services Society, a registered charity in the Vancouver area, leads the programme. Future Bridge Academy is the programme's trading name, and it is delivered with partner non-profit organisations and charities in other Canadian cities.

Where do you run?

The Vancouver area first, from our New Westminster venue, with partner organisations joining in other cities as each one is ready. Every city runs the same programme rather than a lookalike.

When does the centre open?

We do not publish an opening date until it is confirmed, because a date we cannot keep would be worse than no date. Families in the community hear first.

Can I read the site in Turkish?

Not yet. The website is in English today and Turkish versions of the key pages are planned but not built. Our community is largely Turkish-speaking, so write to us in Turkish if that is easier and we will do our best to help.

Can I help?

Yes, and it is the most useful thing anyone can offer us. Mentors, high-school volunteers and teachers all have a role, with documented hours and a reference when you need one.
