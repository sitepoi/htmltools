The programme behind the diploma

Six subjects, a core, and two years to carry them

The Diploma Programme (DP) is the IB's qualification for students aged 16 to 19. It is one programme with two parts: the subjects you choose and the core every student completes.

Students choose from six subject groups: studies in language and literature, language acquisition, individuals and societies, sciences, mathematics and the arts. A student may take a second sciences, individuals and societies or languages subject instead of an arts subject.

Most students take three or four subjects at higher level (240 teaching hours each) and the rest at standard level (150 hours). Higher and standard level are graded on the same scale, but higher level expects a greater body of knowledge and skill - which is why universities look at the combination, not just the total.

The core is what makes the diploma different: theory of knowledge, the extended essay and creativity, activity, service. None of them can be revised the night before, and none of them can be copied from a textbook.

The diploma is awarded by the IB, not by a school and not by a tutor. A school must be authorised as an IB World School to enter candidates - that rule decides what a family can and cannot arrange.

What the diploma actually requires

The whole specification, in one table

No mystery and no marketing: this is the work, and this is how it is measured.

Part What it means Size
Six subject groups Language and literature, language acquisition, individuals and societies, sciences, mathematics, and the arts (or a second subject from another group). 3-4 at higher level, the rest at standard level
Theory of knowledge How we know what we claim to know - assessed through an exhibition and an essay. 1,600-word essay plus an exhibition
Extended essay An independent piece of research on a topic of the student's choice, supervised and externally assessed. 4,000 words, with three reflection sessions
Creativity, activity, service A project and a record across the three strands. Required for the diploma, but not scored. Seven learning outcomes, across two years
Points Each subject is graded 7 to 1. Theory of knowledge and the extended essay together add up to three more. 24 points to be awarded, 45 maximum

Higher level is not a harder label on the same course. Higher level runs 240 teaching hours and standard level 150, and both are graded against the same descriptors. The combination of subjects matters as much as the total.

Why choose the harder option

Because the work leaves a record

Four things the IB asks for that a transcript alone cannot show.

01

Your result is a number

Six subjects at up to 7 points each, plus up to 3 from the core: a total out of 45 that universities can compare with every other IB applicant. More than 4,500 universities in 110+ countries receive IB transcripts.

02

Research becomes a habit

The extended essay is practical preparation for undergraduate research: a question, a method, sources and an argument, held to a word limit. Students meet that demand before university, not during first year.

03

Thinking gets examined

Theory of knowledge asks students to reflect on the nature of knowledge and on how we know what we claim to know. It is assessed - the thinking has to be shown, not described.

04

Language stays alive

Every student studies at least two languages, and a bilingual diploma is available at grade 3 or higher. A second language held for two more years is an asset no short course can fake.

The honest part

What two years of IB actually feel like

Students who can study a lot do well here. Students who wait to be chased do not.

  1. 1

    Six subjects, not three

    Every subject keeps running. There is no year of coasting on the ones you like and abandoning the ones you do not.

  2. 2

    Deadlines that are marked

    Extended essay, theory of knowledge, internal assessments and CAS reflections all land during the two years. They are graded, not collected.

  3. 3

    Writing is continuous

    Essays, lab reports, commentaries, an extended essay. A student who avoids writing will struggle in every subject, not just English.

  4. 4

    The pace is set for you

    Higher level carries 240 teaching hours. Falling behind in October is expensive by March, because the exams are external and fixed.

If a student is already stretched thin, the honest answer is to fix the foundations first. The IB rewards stamina and organisation - it does not create them.

Who it suits

A programme for students who would rather go deep

  • You would rather understand a topic than finish the worksheet
  • You can carry several deadlines without being reminded
  • You are willing to write, and then rewrite
  • You want a second language to stay part of your week
  • You want something to show for service and activity, not just marks

Who it does not suit

A student who is already behind in mathematics or English, or who is carrying too many commitments, will find the IB multiplying the problem rather than fixing it. The diploma is a harder programme - it is not a shortcut to a better average.

Grade 9 and Grade 10

The runway is built before Grade 11

The diploma starts in Grade 11. The habits it needs are built two years earlier - and they help whether or not the student ends up in the IB.

Grade 9

  • Close the gaps. Mathematics, English and science solid. The IB moves fast, and a gap in Grade 9 travels with you into Grade 12.
  • Read longer, write weekly. One page a week, marked and corrected, is what turns into a 4,000-word essay later. Start now, when the stakes are low.
  • Keep the second language. Two languages are required and grades 9 and 10 are the years that make the bilingual diploma possible.
  • Learn how you study. Weekly review beats last-night revision. The IB does not give you a week to catch up between assessments.

Grade 10

  • Choose a direction, not a label. Decide which subjects you would take at higher level, and check what the universities you are aiming for require.
  • Write something longer. An independent project or a research-style essay teaches the moves the extended essay will demand.
  • Start the record. Service, activities, competitions, a part-time job. CAS asks for evidence across two years, not intentions in June.
  • Visit the schools. The DP must be delivered by an authorised IB World School. Ask about entry requirements, subject offerings and deadlines in Grade 10, not Grade 11.

Then, in Grade 11, the runway becomes the programme: higher level choices, extended essay topic, theory of knowledge, CAS and internal assessments - all at once, which is why the habits matter more than the content.

Questions parents ask

The honest answers

How do universities evaluate an IB result?

They look at the total out of 45, the grades in the higher level subjects the degree requires, and the evidence behind the score - the extended essay, theory of knowledge and internal assessments. The IB sends an official transcript directly to universities, up to six free before results day, and each university publishes its own IB requirement and credit policy.

Is the IB harder than the regular programme?

Yes. Six subjects continue at once, the core adds a research paper and a theory of knowledge essay, and the exams are external. The workload is the point: the diploma is evidence that a student carried it.

Do universities prefer IB students?

Universities recognise the IB diploma, and the IB reports that DP students enrol, persist and graduate at higher rates than the national average in a US study, and outperform A-level peers on higher education outcomes in a UK study. Recognition and offers still depend on the university and the programme, so check each one.

Can my child start the IB in Grade 11 without the MYP?

Yes. The Middle Years Programme prepares students well, but it is not a formal entry requirement for the Diploma Programme. Schools set their own admission rules - what actually matters is the foundation in mathematics, English, science and a second language.

What if our school does not offer the IB?

Only an authorised IB World School can deliver the DP. If your school does not offer it, ask the board which nearby schools do, and what their Grade 11 entry looks like. Either way, the Grade 9 and 10 preparation on this page transfers to any demanding programme.

How many points does a student need?

The diploma is awarded at 24 points or more, subject to minimum performance and completion of the core, out of a maximum of 45. Competitive university programmes usually ask for far more than the minimum, and each one publishes its own requirement.

Does the IB limit which universities a student can apply to?

No. More than 4,500 universities in over 110 countries receive IB transcripts. The practical limits come from subject choices and grades, not from the diploma itself - which is why the higher level combination is worth planning in Grade 10.

Is the IB only for students with top marks?

No, but it is for students who can sustain work. A student with strong marks and poor organisation will find the IB punishing; a student with good habits and average marks can do very well. The programme rewards stamina more than brilliance.

What can a Grade 9 or Grade 10 student do now?

Close subject gaps, read and write every week, keep the second language, start a service record, and plan the higher level subjects before the Grade 11 timetable is set. That list is the whole runway on this page.
