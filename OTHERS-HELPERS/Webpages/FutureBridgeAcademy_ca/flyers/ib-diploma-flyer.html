IB Diploma Programme

University-level work.Two years early.

The IB Diploma is two years of serious academic work in Grades 11 and 12: six subjects, a 4,000-word research paper, a second language and a service record. It is examined by the IB and read by universities in more than 110 countries.

Your result is a number

Six subjects marked 1 to 7, plus up to 3 bonus points. The total is out of 45, so universities compare you with every other IB applicant the same way.

You write real research

A 4,000-word extended essay on a question you choose, with a supervisor and three reflection meetings. This is exactly what university assignments look like.

You learn to argue

Theory of knowledge is a marked course about how we know what we claim to know. It changes how you answer essay questions in every subject.

You leave with two languages

Every IB student studies at least two. Grade 3 or higher in both can earn a bilingual diploma - an asset universities can see.

How universities evaluate an IB graduate

Five things they look at. Not one.

All of it comes from the IB, not from the school.

1

The total, out of 45

Six subjects at up to 7 points each, plus up to 3 from theory of knowledge and the extended essay. The diploma is awarded at 24 or more.

2

Your higher level subjects

Three or four subjects at higher level, 240 teaching hours each. Universities check the grades in the subjects your degree needs.

3

The work behind the score

The extended essay, theory of knowledge and internal assessments are marked during the two years, not only in the final exams.

4

The official transcript

The IB sends results straight to universities - up to six, free, when requested before results day.

5

Each university's published policy

Every university states its IB requirement and its credit policy. Check the requirement for the degree you want, not just the diploma.
