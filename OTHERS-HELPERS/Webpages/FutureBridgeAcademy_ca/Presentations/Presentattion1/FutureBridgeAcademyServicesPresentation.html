Self-paced courses
New service 1 of 3

A curriculum becomes a path

For the student who wants to move faster, catch up, or keep learning between live lessons.

  • A course is written as sections; each section holds lessons, and sections unlock in order.
  • The student works at their own pace against a recommended end date.
  • A supervisor follows progress and approves work - no spreadsheets, no chasing.
  • Every quiz answer, status and setting is kept in one saved record for that student.

The content is authored once. Each student's record is separate, so changing the course never erases a student's progress.

Course view: sections unlock in order, with completion and quiz average.

Progress and reports

A supervisor opens one dashboard instead of chasing updates.

Future Bridge Academy 8 / 15
Student files
New service 2 of 3

One student, one file, one plan

A single preparation file for applications and coaching, built with the student and reviewed with a mentor.

  • Every pathway in one place: university, college, IB, trades, high school or other.
  • Readiness is a percentage built from the requirements that are done, not an opinion.
  • Profile, applications, portfolio, achievements, grades, references and documents.
  • The mentor opens the same file and asks for what is missing.

Built for families applying for the first time, who do not yet know what "ready" looks like.

The student file dashboard, with a sample student loaded.

Future Bridge Academy 9 / 15
Student files
How coaching runs

Each pathway, tracked on its own

A university application and a trades application do not need the same checklist - and the file knows the difference.

  • Each pathway carries its own targets, deadlines and completed count.
  • Requests carry an owner, a status, a due date and a conversation.
  • Mentor notes keep the story: what was decided, and what comes next.

Readiness rises as each request closes. The mentor view coaches; the student view does the work.

English article writing
New service 3 of 3

Write, get scored, improve

One focused writing surface for one essay, with evaluation that never rewrites the student's work.

  • The teacher defines the assignment: subject, instructions, minimum word count and rubrics.
  • The student writes, presses Evaluate My Essay, and gets a score with concrete next steps.
  • Teachers can add rubrics - up to eight - and a subject definition can carry its own.
  • The student makes every change. The tool never edits the essay.
Base rubricWeight
Content and ideas30
Organization and structure20
Evidence and argumentation20
Language and style15
Grammar and mechanics15
Total100

Five consistent criteria beat a vague "good essay" comment every time - and the score is feedback, not a replacement for the writing.

English article writing
Inside an evaluation

83.5 out of 100, with the reasons

The evaluation explains the score, criterion by criterion, and leaves the essay untouched.

  • An overall score out of 100, with a grade band from A to F.
  • A summary, strengths and weaknesses, and a comment for every rubric.
  • Three or more concrete next steps, saved with every draft in the student's history.

The student leaves with a list of changes to make - not a rewritten essay.

How a family moves through it

One journey, five optional steps

1Join a free online lesson
2Try a free mock exam
3Take a self-paced course
4Open a student file with a coach
5Write and improve an article
  • Nothing is forced: each step is optional and can stand alone.
  • The free programmes stay free, and no price is published. Where a service carries a fee, it is explained before a family agrees to anything.
  • Anyone can ask to have their details deleted at any time.

The journey is a menu, not a funnel. Families take the steps that fit their year.
