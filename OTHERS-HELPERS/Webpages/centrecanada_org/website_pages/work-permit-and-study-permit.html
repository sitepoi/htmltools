Work Permit and
Study Permit

Two of the most common documents newcomers ask us about. Here is what each one is for, what to prepare, and where to check the current rules before you apply.

Guide Approx. 5 min read CentreCanada team

A work permit allows you to work in Canada; a study permit allows you to study at a designated institution. Both are issued by Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC), and both depend on the reason you are in Canada.

Start with the permit, not the paperwork

Most delays we see are not caused by missing forms — they are caused by applying for the wrong permit. Before you collect documents, confirm which category fits your situation: work, study, or something else entirely. IRCC publishes the current categories and eligibility rules, and they do change.

If you are unsure, bring your current documents to an appointment and we will help you identify what applies to you. We do not decide your eligibility, but we can help you read the requirements and prepare a complete file.

What people usually need to prepare

  • A valid passport and any current permits or visas
  • Proof of the purpose of your stay — a job offer, an acceptance letter, or a program
  • Proof of funds, where the category requires it
  • Photographs to the current specification
  • Supporting documents such as educational records or language test results
  • Any prior IRCC correspondence, including refusal letters

Keep originals and clear scans of everything. If a document is not in English or French, it usually needs a certified translation.

A refusal is not the end of the road

Many applications are refused for a fixable reason: a missing document, an unclear financial statement, or an application submitted in the wrong category. Read the refusal letter carefully — it usually tells you what was missing.

Applying online, and why details matter

Applications are submitted through IRCC's online portal. Small inconsistencies — a date that does not match your passport, an address that changed between forms — slow the file down more often than people expect. Fill everything in once, carefully, and keep a copy of exactly what you sent.
