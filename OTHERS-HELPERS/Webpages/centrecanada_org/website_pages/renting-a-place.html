Renting a Place

Finding a home is one of the first big tasks after arriving. Here is how the renting process usually works in British Columbia, and what to check before you sign anything.

Housing guide Approx. 5 min read CentreCanada team

Renting in Canada is a process with steps: you find a place, view it, apply, sign a tenancy agreement and pay a deposit. Knowing the order helps you avoid pressure and scams.

1. Find places that fit your budget

Listings appear on rental websites, community notice boards, cultural associations and through word of mouth. Before you start viewing, decide two numbers: the monthly rent you can afford, and the total cash you can pay up front.

Rent is rarely the only cost. Ask about utilities (heat, electricity, water, internet), parking, laundry and whether a storage locker is included.

2. View the place in person

  • Check for damp, mould, pests and working smoke alarms
  • Look at water pressure, heating and windows
  • Ask what furniture and appliances are included
  • Confirm transit, schools and grocery stores nearby
  • Write down anything broken and confirm it will be fixed

Never send money before you have viewed the place

If a landlord asks for a deposit before a viewing, refuses to meet in person, or pushes you to decide within minutes, stop. Deposits are paid after a tenancy agreement is agreed — not to hold a listing you have never seen.

3. Apply with the documents landlords ask for

A typical rental application asks for identification, proof of income or employment, and references. Newcomers who do not yet have Canadian credit history can offer a letter of employment, a bank statement, a reference from a previous landlord abroad, or a co-signer — ask the landlord what they will accept before you apply.

4. Sign a written tenancy agreement

In British Columbia, a tenancy agreement should be in writing and include the parties, the address, the rent, the date rent is due, and what services and utilities are included. Read it before signing, and keep a copy.

Ask about the length of the term, notice periods, rules about guests and pets, and how repairs are requested.

5. Deposits and the condition inspection

Deposits are regulated. Under BC's Residential Tenancy Act a security deposit is generally half of one month's rent, and a separate pet damage deposit may also apply. A written condition inspection should be completed when you move in — keep that record with your agreement.

Confirm the current rules and amounts with the Residential Tenancy Branch, since details can change.

6. Protect yourself after you move in

  • Get tenant insurance — it usually covers your belongings, not just the building
  • Keep receipts for rent and any deposits
  • Report repairs in writing so there is a record
  • Know where to ask for help if a dispute starts

Official sources and tenant support

  • Residential Tenancy Branch — Government of BC
  • Tenant Resource & Advisory Centre (TRAC)

This guide is general information, not legal advice. For a specific dispute or agreement, contact the Residential Tenancy Branch or a tenant advocate. CentreCanada can help you understand a document and find the right service.
