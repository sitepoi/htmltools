This handbook is yours

Keep it next to your phone. Everything you learned in the seminar is here - how to ask, what AI can do for you, and how to stay safe.

You do not need to become technical. You only need to learn to ask - and the computer does the heavy lifting. Ten minutes of practice a day keeps the skill alive, and the prompts in this book are ready to copy.

The promise You will be able to: open a chat with AI, ask a useful question, get a helpful answer in plain language, know when an answer may be wrong, and stay safe from AI-powered scams.

AI in plain words

Imagine a friend who has read every book, article, and recipe in the world - in every language. This friend cannot feel, does not know you personally, and sometimes remembers things wrong. But this friend is extremely fast at summarizing, explaining, writing, translating, and giving ideas. That friend is AI.

  • AI is not a person. It has no feelings, opinions, or plans of its own - it answers what you ask, nothing more.
  • AI does not know you. It only knows what you type. You decide what it ever learns about you.
  • AI is not always right. It can make mistakes with complete confidence - like some voices on the internet. Anything that matters gets checked.

And AI is already working for you: the junk-mail filter, the map app, the bank alert that spots an unusual purchase, and the voice assistant in your phone.

What comes next

AI keeps getting better at talking, reading forms, and translating - and in the coming years it will live in every phone, car, and home. Your part stays the same: ask clearly, check what matters, and never type secrets. The five golden rules will still protect you in 2035.

Good news AI has no power over you. It only has the power you choose to give it.

How to ask well

A vague question gets a vague answer. A rich question gets a useful answer. The recipe has four parts:

Part 1

Who

Who you are, and who this is for.

Part 2

What

What you want, exactly.

Part 3

Details

What matters to you.

Part 4

Format

How the answer should look.

Before: "write letter landlord" - a vague, useless letter.
After: "I am a 74-year-old tenant. My tap has dripped for two weeks and I called the manager twice. Write a short, polite letter to my landlord asking for a repair, under 150 words." - a ready-to-send letter.

If the answer is not right, use one of the five fix-up words:

Shorter Simpler Step by step With an example Another version

You can also say "answer me in my own language" - the chat apps answer in your language too.

Your own folders: Projects

ChatGPT and Claude both have Projects - folders for one subject. Keep a Health folder, a Family folder, a Garden folder. The AI reads everything in the folder and answers with that background - no repeating yourself.

Make it personal: in the folder settings add one line, for example "I am 72, explain simply, answer me in Turkish." Every chat in that folder follows it.

What AI remembers

  • ChatGPT memory: it can save things you tell it - your name, your city, that you like short answers - and use them next time. Check what it saved by asking: "What do you remember about me?" Delete anything under Settings, Personalization, Memory - or just say "Forget everything."
  • Claude: remembers only inside the current chat. To make it remember across chats, put the facts in a Project.
  • The rule: let it remember preferences, never secrets. A memory of "likes short answers" is helpful; a memory of a bank card number must never exist.

Make it better by talking

The first answer is a first draft, not the final one. You and AI improve it together - and you can do it all by talking:

  1. Ask for a draft. One clear question.
  2. Say: "Read it back to me." You hear what you have.
  3. Say one change. "Warmer", "shorter", "remove the second paragraph".
  4. Hear the new version. Then say the next change. Repeat until it sounds like you.

Useful words: shorter, simpler, warmer, more polite, more formal, two versions, read it back, translate it back.

Try it by voice Tap the microphone and say: "Read my letter back to me, then suggest three small improvements - and only make the ones I choose."

Letters, papers, and planning

Write it

Complaints, thank-you notes, invitations, appointment requests - drafted in one minute, then you edit and send.

Type this Write a polite complaint letter to my internet company. My internet has been very slow for a week, I called three times, and nothing changed. I would like a refund for this month. Keep the letter short and clear.

Understand it

Paste a difficult letter from the bank, insurance, or government and ask for plain words:

Type this Please explain this letter in plain words, like you are talking to a friend who is not a lawyer. Then tell me, step by step, what I need to DO and any deadlines I must not miss: [paste the letter]

Plan it

Type this I have a busy week coming up: a doctor appointment on Tuesday, a pharmacy visit on Wednesday, and a family birthday dinner on Saturday. Help me plan my week day by day, with one reminder for each day, in a simple table.

Do not like typing? Tap the microphone in the app and speak instead. Or say "Hey Siri" / "Hey Google" on your phone for reminders and timers.

Food: ingredients, allergies, halal

AI can read an ingredient list with you - but it cannot taste, cannot test, and cannot certify anything. The label and the certification mark stay the final word.

  • Explain the label: paste any ingredient list and get each item in plain words.
  • Allergy check: name your allergens, paste the list - AI flags possible problems, then the food maker confirms.
  • Halal check: AI explains which ingredients may come from animal sources or alcohol and what to ask - the official halal certification label remains the reliable confirmation.
  • Vegetarian, vegan, and other diets: name your diet and paste the list - AI tells you what fits and what does not, in plain words.
Type this I am checking food for allergies. My allergens are peanuts and eggs. Please check this ingredient list and explain anything I should worry about: [paste the list]
Type this I follow this diet: [gluten-free / vegan / low sugar]. Please check this ingredient list and tell me which items fit and which do not: [paste the list]
Remember For allergies, ask the maker directly. For halal, look for the certification mark. For personal health effects, ask your doctor.

Health and medicines

The one rule that matters AI gives ideas and explanations. It never decides anything medical. No diagnosis, no dose changes, no emergencies. Doctors, nurses, and pharmacists remain the only health authority.

Meals, movement, schedules

Type this I am 70 years old and my doctor asked me to eat less salt. Give me five simple low-salt dinner ideas, each with fewer than eight ingredients. Do not give medical advice.

Understand your medicine

Type this Please help me understand my medicine in plain words. From my label: [medicine name and dose]. Explain what it is usually for, how it is commonly taken, and common side effects. Then list five good questions to ask my pharmacist.

Doctor visits

  • Before: ask AI to prepare what to tell the doctor and five questions to ask.
  • During: bring your printed medication list. Language trouble? Open ChatGPT Voice (best) or Microsoft Copilot voice and hand the phone over.
  • After: tell AI what you remember, in your own words - it rewrites it clearly with what to do next.

Health news: true or false?

  1. Who says it? A named doctor or hospital, or "a friend of a friend"?
  2. Where is the proof? Studies and official sources, or screenshots?
  3. Check an official source. MedlinePlus, WHO, or your country's health authority.
  4. Watch for red flags. "Miracle cure", "secret formula", "doctors hate this", "act now" - stop and verify.
  5. Ask a professional. Your doctor or pharmacist settles it.

Healthy aging: tools and benefits

  • Meals and movement - ChatGPT (best) or Claude: menus and seated routines within your doctor's limits. Benefit: independence without guesswork.
  • Medicine reminders - Medisafe, MyTherapy, or your phone's reminders: the right pill at the right time. Benefit: no forgotten doses.
  • Steps and activity - Apple Health or Google Fit: your phone counts them for you. Benefit: you see progress and keep moving.
  • Mind and company - ChatGPT or Claude: daily memory games and calm chat. Benefit: a sharper mind and company on quiet days.
  • Brain games - Lumosity, Peak, or Elevate: a few free games a day. Benefit: a daily mental workout.
  • Hearing and vision - live captions and Be My Eyes: live captions turn spoken words into text on the screen; Be My Eyes reads labels aloud. Benefit: you take part in everything.
  • Living-alone check-in - Snug: one tap a day; family is alerted if you miss it. Benefit: peace of mind for everyone.

These tools build the habit. Every plan still gets your doctor's approval.

Family and translation

AI keeps you close to the people you love - birthday wishes, homework help with grandchildren, bedtime stories, and letters across languages.

Type this Write a warm birthday message for my 10-year-old grandson who loves dinosaurs and football. Short and cheerful, about 60 words.

Live translation

  • Live conversation - two good choices:
    • ChatGPT Voice (best): tap the voice button and say "Be my translator between English and [language]". Talk naturally - it listens and speaks both ways like a real person.
    • Microsoft Copilot voice: works too, and is already on many Windows computers.
    Google Translate Conversation mode remains a backup, but many people find it stiff and slow next to AI voice.
  • Camera translation: Google Lens and the Translate camera read menus, signs, medicine boxes, and letters.
  • Letters: paste any letter into DeepL or a chat app for a natural translation in both directions.
For doctor visits Open an AI voice translator before the appointment - ChatGPT Voice is best, Copilot works too - and hand the phone to the doctor when needed. Short, clear sentences translate best.

Hobbies, memories, and your projects

  • Gardening: "I live in Ontario and want to grow tomatoes on my balcony. Give me a simple month-by-month plan."
  • Heritage recipes: ask AI to write down a family recipe as a keepsake, with the story behind it.
  • Photos: Google Photos "Magic Editor" and iPhone "Clean Up" repair old pictures; ask AI for beautiful captions.
  • Your life story: "Interview me one question at a time, starting with my childhood home, then write my answers into a story."
  • Big dreams: a book, a small shop, a course you could teach - AI handles the spelling, design, and speed. You bring the wisdom.

Stay safe: the golden rules

  1. No secrets in the chat. Never passwords, bank or card numbers, ID numbers, or full addresses.
  2. Never send money because of a call or message. No real bank, police officer, or grandchild ever demands gift cards or urgent transfers.
  3. AI does not call you first. A call about your account, your computer, or "AI support" is a scam. Hang up.
  4. Verify before you trust. Check important facts with official sources - the doctor, the bank's own website, the government.
  5. When in doubt, stop. If you feel rushed or scared, end it and talk to family first.

Never type these

  • Passwords and PINs.
  • Bank and card numbers, expiry dates, the code on the back.
  • Passport, social insurance, and health card numbers.
  • Your full address and exact birth date - age and city are enough.
  • Other people's full names, addresses, phone numbers, and photos of children.
  • Medical files that carry your name and ID together.

One test for anything: would I read this out loud in a public library? If not, keep it out of the chat.

The family code word

Scammers can copy a family member's voice with AI and call: "Grandma, I am in trouble, I need money now." Agree on a secret word only your family knows. Any caller claiming to be family must say it - a perfect fake voice still cannot know it.

Remember People who know these five rules and one code word are safer than most internet users of any age. The goal is confidence, not fear.

Ten minutes a day

5 minutes

One real task

A shopping list, an email, a recipe - something actually needed.

2 minutes

One fix-up

Use one fix-up word on the answer.

3 minutes

One curious question

A bird, a song, a place, a word.

Bonus

Teach a friend

Teaching is the proof you learned.

Small regular use beats long sessions. In ten weeks, that is seventy real tasks.

Your helper app

After the seminar, you get the Senior Helper AI Coordinator - one friendly app with a button for everything you learned. No typing needed: tap a button, fill the simple boxes, and the helper asks the AI for you.

  • Write a letter - fill four boxes, the letter appears.
  • Explain a paper - paste a difficult letter, get plain words.
  • Food and ingredients - allergy check, halal check, ingredient explainer.
  • Pharmacy and medicines - understand medicines, check health claims.
  • Doctor visit helper - questions before, plain summary after.
  • Translate - letters, phrases, and words for a doctor visit.
  • Check a suspicious message - paste it, the helper checks for scams.
  • My saved answers - keep the answers you want to use again.

The five golden rules stay on screen at all times, and there is a Listen button that reads answers out loud.

Volunteer: teach the next generation

Your decades of experience can teach young people - and you do not need to be a teacher. CentreCanada has the tools; you bring the knowledge.

  1. Chat with AI about your subject. A trade, a homeland, a hobby, a recipe tradition - anything worth teaching.
  2. Let AI draft the lesson. It writes the topic, a friendly explanation, examples, and quiz questions.
  3. Print it as a PDF. One click turns the draft into a shareable file.
  4. Hand it to the team. We upload it into the curriculum builder, which turns it into training flows, quizzes, and web documents for students. Your name goes on it as the contributor.
The volunteer prompt I would like to volunteer a lesson for young students about [your subject]. Interview me one question at a time about the most important things a beginner should know. Then write a complete draft education document: the topic, a friendly explanation, three examples, and five quiz questions with answers.
One hour a week No technical skill needed - the AI does the formatting. Talk to the course team if you would like to join.

Flyers and posters for your cause

AI writes the words, Canva makes the design - free and in minutes.

Type this We are organizing a community dinner for [cause]. Write the short flyer text: a warm headline, three lines about the event, and one line asking people to bring a dish. Then give me a design brief for Canva: A4, warm colors, large text, space for our logo.

Paste the design brief into Canva - Magic Design - it builds the flyer, poster, or presentation, and you edit it. The same trick works for birthday invitations and community talks.

Tricks for ChatGPT

  • Letters: after the first draft, say "Shorter, and warmer."
  • Plain words: "Explain like I am 12, with an example from daily life."
  • Voice translator: tap the voice button and say "Be my translator between English and [language]."
  • Company: "Let's have a ten-minute chat about my day. Ask me one question at a time."
  • Scams: paste the message and ask "Is this a scam? What warning signs does it show?"

Tricks for Claude

  • Long letters: paste anything and ask "Summarize in five bullets and tell me what I must DO."
  • Warm writing: "Write this card like a warm grandmother - sincere and short."
  • Life story: "Interview me one question at a time, starting with my childhood home."
  • Your own draft: "Rewrite this keeping my voice, and keep it under 200 words."
  • Comparing choices: "Compare these two options in a simple table."
