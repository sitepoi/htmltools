The one idea to remember

AI is the library friend

Imagine a friend who has read every book, article, and recipe in the world - in every language.

  • Extremely fast at summarizing, explaining, writing, and translating.
  • Never tired, never impatient, happy to repeat anything.
  • Does not think like a person - it guesses the next word, again and again, so well that it looks like thinking.

That friend is AI. You do not need to know how it works - just like you drive a car without knowing the engine.

Straight talk

What AI is not

It is not a person

No feelings, no opinions, no plans of its own. It answers what you ask - that is the whole job.

It does not know you

It only sees what you type in the chat. You decide what it ever learns about you.

It is not always right

It can state a mistake with full confidence - like some voices on the internet. Anything that matters gets checked.

So treat it like any source you meet online: take the helpful ideas, keep your own judgment. AI has no power over you - only the power you choose to give it.

The whole skill, on one slide

Ask well, and AI does the rest

A vague question gets a vague answer. A rich question gets a useful answer. The recipe has four parts:

Part 1

Who

Who are you, and who is this for?

"I am a 75-year-old grandmother..."

Part 2

What

What do you want, exactly?

"...I need a short birthday letter for my grandson..."

Part 3

Details

What matters to you?

"...he loves dinosaurs and football..."

Part 4

Format

How should the answer look?

"...about 60 words, warm and simple."

Remember it as: Who - What - Details - Format.

Tap to open - complete sample prompts
Sample 1 - a birthday letter Who: I am a 75-year-old grandmother. What: I need a short birthday letter for my grandson. Details: he loves dinosaurs and football. Format: about 60 words, warm and simple.
Sample 2 - a doctor visit Who: I am a 68-year-old patient. What: help me prepare for my appointment. Details: I take three medicines and my knees hurt. Format: a list of what to tell the doctor and five questions to ask.
Sample 3 - a week of dinners Who: I am cooking for one. What: plan five low-salt dinners. Details: easy, under eight ingredients each. Format: a table plus one shopping list.
Tap to open - make it better by talking

The first answer is a first draft. Improve it by talking - one spoken change at a time:

  • "Read it back to me" - you hear the draft.
  • "Warmer", "shorter", "remove the second paragraph" - one change per round.
  • "Give me two versions - one formal, one warm" - then pick.
  • "What would make this better? Three ideas" - the AI becomes your editor.
The loop in one line Draft - listen - say one change - listen again. Repeat until it sounds like you.

The true-or-false check for health news:

  • Who says it - a named doctor or hospital, or "a friend of a friend"?
  • Where is the proof - studies and official sources, or screenshots?
  • Check MedlinePlus or WHO, then ask your doctor.
  • Red flags: "miracle cure", "secret formula", "doctors hate this", "act now".

Safety first - read slowly

The five golden rules

  1. No secrets in the chat. Never passwords, bank or card numbers, ID numbers, or full addresses.
  2. Never send money because of a call or message. No real bank, police officer, or grandchild ever demands gift cards or urgent transfers.
  3. AI does not call you first. A call about your account, your computer, or "AI support" is a scam. Hang up.
  4. Verify before you trust. Important facts get checked against official sources - the doctor, the bank's own website, the government.
  5. When in doubt, stop. Urgency is the scammer's tool. If you feel rushed or scared, end it and talk to family first.
Know the tricks - week 6

Scams that use AI

The voice-clone call

A scammer copies a family member's voice from a public video and calls: "Grandma, I am in trouble, I need money now, do not tell mom."

Your answer: the family code word

Agree on a secret word only your family knows. Any caller claiming to be family must say it. A perfect voice still cannot know it.

Fake messages and emails

Your bank, the post office, a "missed delivery" - with a link and urgency. Never tap links in unexpected messages.

Fake photos and videos

Deepfakes can show people doing things that never happened. Check with the person directly before believing or forwarding.

The goal is confidence, not fear. People who know these five rules and one code word are safer than most internet users of any age.
